We rate the stock SELL. Our framework is BUY above +15% expected 12-month total return, SELL below –10%, and HOLD in between. We use total return rather than price upside because SITC pays out 80-85% of its earnings, so dividends are a large part of what a holder receives.

**Variant view.** The share price capitalises 2026 as a normal year. It is not. SITC's average freight rate rose to US$865/TEU in Q3 2026 (our derivation from the 1H and 9M figures), 21% above Q3 2025. In our judgement much of that came from the fuel and freight spike that followed the disruption around the Strait of Hormuz from late February 2026 (Drewry, FreightWaves), and Drewry reported intra-Asia rates softening from late July. The global container order book equals about 40% of the fleet (Alphaliner, 25-Oct-2026, our calculation), and Linerlytica expected 2.9m TEU of deliveries in 2027 and 3.8m TEU in 2028 (September 2025 estimate). SITC also takes delivery of most of its own 66,000 TEU order book over the same two years. In our base case the average rate falls 10% in FY2027E and 5% in FY2028E, to US$711/TEU, which is close to the FY2024-25 average of US$737. Attributable profit falls from US$1,472m in FY2026E to US$1,184m in FY2027E and US$1,049m in FY2028E.

**What is priced.** Holding our FY2027E-30E cash flows, WACC and terminal growth, the current price requires terminal-year free cash flow of US$1.43bn. That is 42% above our FY2030E figure of US$1.00bn: about US$296 of free cash flow per TEU lifted, against US$209 in our FY2030E and US$336 in FY2026E, a strong year. At our base-case P/E of 10.9x, the price implies EPS of HK$4.39. That is above our FY2026E EPS of HK$4.27, which comes from a year of disruption-boosted rates. Consensus expects EPS to fall by about 2% a year over three years (S&P Global MI via stockanalysis.com). We expect a 29% fall from FY2026E to FY2028E. The gap is in freight rates, not in volumes or costs.

**The strongest case against us.** SITC has earned high returns through every cycle we can see in its filings: ROE was 26% even in the 2023 trough. It holds US$0.9bn of net cash and pays most of its profit out, and the Q3 2026 rate shows no weakness yet. Disruption premia have lasted longer than expected before: the Red Sea diversions that began in late 2023 were still in place in 2026. If the Hormuz premium persists into 2027, the bull case (rates –3% in 2027, then flat) supports HK$59.40, above today's price. **Verdict: later, not wrong.** The bull case needs rates to hold through a delivery wave of about 6.7m TEU in 2027-28, while regional peers hold order books equal to 64% of their fleets. We weight it at 25%. The probability-weighted DCF value of HK$36.99 is still 22% below the price.

## 2. Discounted cash flow

**Method.** Unlevered free cash flow is EBIT excluding disposal gains × (1 – tax) + D&A – capex (including newbuild instalments) + disposal proceeds – increase in working capital – lease payments (principal and interest). We treat leases as operating costs: lease payments are deducted from cash flow and lease liabilities are not deducted in the bridge. The valuation date is 31-Dec-2026, so net cash is taken after the HK$1.00 special dividend paid on 17-Dec-2026. Cash flows from FY2027E are discounted mid-year. FY2026E is not discounted because 10 of its 12 months have already passed and its cash is in the year-end net cash figure.

SITC trades at about six times the peer median P/B and twice the median EV/EBITDA. Its ROE is four to five times the median. On P/B and EV/EBITDA the peer set says nothing useful about SITC's value: peer median P/B on our YE2026E book would give HK$7.27, and peer median EV/EBITDA on FY2026E EBITDA gives HK$28.07. Earnings are the fairer basis. The premium also sits in the P/E: SITC's 11.7x forward P/E is above the 75th percentile (10.9x) and 50% above the median (7.8x), while consensus expects peer EPS to fall at a median 13% a year over three years and SITC's to fall 2% a year.

**Applying the multiples.** We value SITC at the 75th percentile forward P/E (10.9x) on our FY2027E EPS of HK$3.42, which gives **HK$37.15**. The top-quartile multiple reflects the highest ROE and payout in the group. It is also close to the P/E that a Gordon model gives for an 80% payout at a 10.1% cost of equity and 2% growth: 0.80 × 1.02 / 0.081 = 10.1x. Our range runs from the median, 7.8x (HK$26.68), to 13x (HK$44.47), which is SITC's trailing P/E. Scaling the peer median P/B by SITC's FY2027E ROE relative to the peer median gives 3.8x book, or HK$30.10.

We do not use precedent transactions. SITC has a controlling shareholder: the Yang Shaopeng family trust holds 40.49%. No listed regional container line has changed control in recent years. The last large deal in the sector, COSCO's acquisition of OOIL in 2017-18, was priced at a different point in the cycle and for a long-haul network. A control premium is not part of our 12-month thesis.

## 8. Sanity checks and data notes

- **Balance and tie-out.** The model balances in every year and every scenario, and every historical total ties to the filings (Checks tab). The DCF tab's cash flows tie to the DCF Inputs tab, and the centre of each sensitivity grid equals the DCF value.
- **WACC.** At 10.1% it is within the 7-12% range for a mature cyclical. The 5% risk-free rate is high by recent standards. At a 4% risk-free rate the WACC would be about 9.1% and the DCF value about HK$40.90, still 14% below the price.
- **Terminal assumptions.** 2% growth and a 9.7x implied EV/EBITDA exit are conservative for a 30%-margin, net-cash business, and generous for a cyclical one. Table 1 shows the range.
- **Peer data.** The multiples come from a single public aggregator and were not checked against each company's filings. Hapag-Lloyd's 389x LTM P/E and ZIM's 3.2x forward P/E are outliers, so we use medians and percentiles, not means.
- **Consensus.** The consensus target and rating are third-party figures, shown for context only.
- **Rounding.** The price target is rounded to the nearest HK$0.50 from HK$36.59.
