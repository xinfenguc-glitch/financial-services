## 1. Company overview

SHEIN Global Holdings Ltd ("SHEIN", "the Company") is the world's largest online fashion destination by apparel and footwear retail sales value. China Insights Consultancy ("CIC"), the industry consultant engaged for the IPO, ranks it the third-largest apparel and footwear company globally, with a 1.9% share of a US$1.74trn market in 2025. Only a US athletic-wear leader (3.0%) and a Spanish fast-fashion group (2.5%) are larger; a German athletic brand (1.7%) and a Japanese casual-wear retailer (1.3%) follow. In 2025 SHEIN generated net revenues of US$41.8bn (+8.0% YoY), operating income of US$1.71bn (4.1% margin) and IFRS net income of US$2.06bn. It served about 273 million active customers across roughly 160 markets and fulfilled 1.08 billion orders. Net revenues compounded at 14.2% a year over 2023-25 and active customers at 21.2%.

The business is a vertically integrated online fashion brand collection, led by first-party sales, with a growing marketplace attached. SHEIN designs products with 370+ in-house designers and a network of contract manufacturers and independent designers. It commissions production from more than 7,500 contract manufacturers, mostly small and medium-sized firms in the Pearl River Delta, stores the output mainly in central warehouses in mainland China, and ships it directly to consumers through its own apps and websites. It does not run physical stores at scale. Revenue is recognised on delivery, and most customers pay in advance. Combined with supplier terms of about 34 days and inventory turnover of about 36 days, prepayment gives SHEIN negative working capital (–US$5.0bn at 30 June 2026) and a large cash balance.

SHEIN calls its core operating model LATR, for Large-scale Automated Test and Reorder. New styles launch in batches of roughly 100-200 units. The Company measures customer response in real time, reorders the styles that sell (restocking in as few as five days) and drops the ones that do not. SHEIN argues that LATR solves the fashion industry's "trilemma" of assortment breadth, design-refresh speed and inventory efficiency, and the numbers support the claim. More than 2 million apparel styles were live at 31 March 2026, customers could find about 4,700 new apparel styles a day in 1Q26, inventory turnover was 36 days in 2025, and inventory write-downs have run at only about 1-2% of cost of sales.

SHEIN earns revenue in two ways. Product revenues (US$37.1bn in 2025, 88.7% of the total) come from first-party sales of company brands (SHEIN, MOTF, Glowmode, SHEGLAM, MUSERA, Aloruh and others) and partnership brands. Service revenues (US$4.7bn, 11.3%) are commissions, generally 10-20% of transaction value, and fulfilment fees charged to third-party merchants on the marketplace launched in 2022-23, plus fees from brand-enablement partners. Service revenue is recognised net, so a rising marketplace mix lowers reported revenue per order and raises fulfilment costs as a percentage of revenue. Investors need to adjust for this when reading the margins.

Revenue is spread across regions. In 2025 the United States contributed 24.1% of net revenues (US$10.1bn), Europe 35.4% (US$14.8bn) and the rest of the world 40.5% (US$16.9bn, led by Latin America and the Middle East). By category, apparel was 63.8% and other categories (footwear, accessories, beauty, home and lifestyle) 36.2%. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and the top five suppliers account for about 16% of purchases.

SHEIN has been headquartered in Singapore since late 2021, is incorporated in the Cayman Islands, and operates through subsidiaries in China, Singapore, the US, Ireland, the UK and the UAE. It employed 17,585 people at 30 June 2026, about 79% of them in mainland China. The Company listed in Hong Kong on 1 September 2026 under a weighted-voting-rights (WVR) structure. It raised HK$13.6bn gross (HK$13.2bn net) by selling 279,992,500 new Class B shares at HK$48.56, a valuation of about US$26bn, roughly a quarter of the ~US$100bn private valuation it reached in 2022. The shares have since fallen 36.5% to HK$30.84, taking the fully diluted market capitalisation to about US$17.4bn.

Investors are debating four issues:
1. The removal of de minimis and other low-value import exemptions in the US (May and August 2025) and the EU (1 July 2026), which raises landed costs on the direct-from-China model.
2. Margin compression in 2026. Operating margin fell to 2.4% in 1H26 from 5.2% in 1H25 as freight rates spiked during the Iran conflict and the Company chose to absorb the costs.
3. Regulatory exposure in the EU and US: the EU Digital Services Act investigation, the FTC investigation, and French and Irish data and consumer proceedings.
4. Governance and capital allocation, after about US$3.5bn of cash was paid to preferred shareholders around the listing.

Against this, SHEIN has US$13.5bn of pro forma unrestricted cash (about 78% of its market value; US$13.9bn including investments), order volumes that are still growing (+6.4% in 1H26), customers up 14.6% over the last twelve months to 291m, and a dividend policy targeting at least 50% of annual net profit.

## 2. Company history

**Origins (2008-2014).** Press accounts trace SHEIN's roots to a cross-border e-commerce business that Xu Yangtian, a search-engine-marketing specialist, started in Nanjing in 2008 to sell apparel to overseas buyers online. The Prospectus dates the formal launch to 2012, when Mr Xu and three co-founders, Miao Miao, Gu Xiaoqing and Ren Xiaoqing, started the venture. All four had worked together at a company that provided search-engine marketing to Chinese exporters. SHEIN was therefore built by digital marketers rather than by merchants or designers, and its early edge came from performance marketing, social-media and influencer engagement, and data-driven merchandising. The SHEIN brand was introduced in 2013, and the Cayman holding company (then named Elite Depot Limited) was incorporated on 12 May 2014.

**Building the machine (2014-2019).** SHEIN pilot-tested LATR in 2014 and launched its mobile app in 2015. In these years it built its operations in Guangzhou, inside the dense garment-manufacturing cluster of the Pearl River Delta, and gradually digitised its suppliers by giving them free cloud-based production-management software. Funding followed the model's traction: a Series A in 2015 (US$5m at a US$53m pre-money valuation), a Series B from 2016 (RMB169m at RMB1.1bn) and a Series C in 2018-19 (US$243m at US$2.4bn). Annual active customers reached 10 million in 2018.

**Hyper-growth (2020-2022).** The pandemic shift to online shopping, combined with marketing built for TikTok and Instagram, made SHEIN a global brand. Active customers passed 50 million in 2020 and 100 million in 2021. That year SHEIN launched the SHEIN X designer-incubation programme and moved its headquarters to Singapore. A Series C+ in March 2020 valued the Company at US$5bn, and a Series D in 2022 raised US$1.8bn at a US$98.2bn pre-money valuation (US$23.72 per share, split-adjusted). Net revenues exceeded US$20bn in 2022. The valuation peaked that year.

**Marketplace, scrutiny and re-rating (2023-2025).** SHEIN formally launched its marketplace for third-party merchants in 2023 and raised US$1.7bn in a Series D+ at a US$64.0bn pre-money valuation, a 35% down-round. It also took a roughly one-third stake in SPARC Group, then the operator of brands including Forever 21, in exchange for Class B shares. The partnership was meant to combine SPARC's physical retail with SHEIN's on-demand supply chain; the investment has since produced cumulative associate losses. Temu's push into Western markets in 2023 led to litigation in both directions: Temu's US antitrust complaint and SHEIN's UK copyright claims. Revenue grew 41.1% in 2023 and 20.7% in 2024, and active customers passed 200 million in 2024. A planned New York listing stalled amid US political scrutiny, a London attempt also stalled, and the Company eventually filed in Hong Kong. Growth slowed to 8.0% in 2025 after the US removed the de minimis exemption for Chinese imports (effective 2 May 2025) and then for all countries (29 August 2025); US revenue fell 3.5%. In October 2025 SHEIN turned SHEIN X into the SHEIN Xcelerator Programme, a brand-enablement platform. Regulatory costs rose, with a €40m DGCCRF settlement in France (July 2025) and a €150m CNIL cookie-consent fine (September 2025, under appeal).

**Road to listing (2026).** In March 2026 the preferred shareholders agreed to move their redemption date from 4 March to 31 December 2026. In exchange they received about US$1.1bn of 8% p.a. distributions (paid March-September 2026), a 12% p.a. coupon from 5 March to listing (about US$230m) and a "Conversion Adjustment" for Series Pre-D, D and D+ holders, similar to a full ratchet. The adjustment was settled at listing with US$2.2bn of cash plus 19.6 million additional shares. The EU approved the end of the €150 customs-duty exemption in February 2026, effective 1 July 2026, with a flat €3 duty per item category. In the second quarter SHEIN acquired Everlane, a US premium basics brand, for US$78m (mostly repayment of Everlane debt). The Hong Kong IPO priced in late August 2026 at HK$48.56, near the mid-point of the HK$47.60-49.50 range. The retail tranche was 5.63x covered and the international book 2.59x. Seven cornerstone investors took 22.1% of the offer; most were existing shareholders (Boyu, Tiger Global, General Atlantic, a Tencent affiliate, Greenwoods), joined by Taikang Life and UBS AM Singapore. The shares opened flat and fell as low as HK$43.72 on the first day, then slid through September. The stabilising manager bought back the full 15% over-allocation at HK$35.90-48.56, and the over-allotment option lapsed on 26 September. On 28 September SHEIN reported its first interim results (1H26 revenue +1.0%, operating income –52.9%). The stock fell 10.7% the next day and closed at a record low of HK$30.84 on 30 September.

## 3. Management team and governance

SHEIN is still run and controlled by its founders. All four co-founders sit on the seven-member Board as executive directors, and their Class A shares, which carry ten votes each, give them 83.3% of the votes on ordinary resolutions. The CFO and CTO are the two professional executives named in the Prospectus as senior management.

### Xu Yangtian, Founder, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (age 43)

Mr Xu founded SHEIN and has been chairman, executive director and CEO since the start, responsible for group strategy and business management. Before SHEIN he worked with his co-founders at a company that provided search-engine marketing to Chinese exporters. He holds a bachelor's degree in international trade from Qingdao University of Technology (2007). That background explains much of how SHEIN works: close attention to customer-acquisition cost, rapid A/B testing and data-driven decisions, later applied to merchandising through LATR. Mr Xu has given almost no interviews, and the Chairman's Statement in the Interim Report is one of his few public written statements. In it he describes SHEIN as a "richly diversified brand collection" built on a "vertically integrated, first-party-led" model and commits that the first-party business "will always remain at the core". He sets three priorities for the next one to two years: broadening the price range toward higher-priced brands, investing more in quality and compliance, and communicating more actively with consumers through brand marketing. He also defends the decision to absorb higher freight costs in 2Q26 instead of raising prices, calling the costs transitory. After the IPO Mr Xu beneficially owns 798,973,148 Class A and 487,090,102 Class B shares, 30.3% of the share capital and 49.9% of the votes, through Apex Sight Holdings, which he controls through a family trust. As the largest WVR beneficiary and the controlling shareholder, he effectively decides board composition and strategy. His holdings are locked up for 24 months. He has built a business with more than US$40bn of net revenues within about 13 years of the 2012 launch, and his style is private and controlling. Combining the CEO and chairman roles is a disclosed deviation from Hong Kong's Corporate Governance Code.

### Miao Miao, Co-Founder, Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer (age 41)

Ms Miao is a co-founder, has been an executive director since the start and has been chief operating officer since 2017, overseeing day-to-day operations. She is also a director or senior manager of several subsidiaries. Like Mr Xu she previously worked at the search-engine-marketing firm where the founders met, and she holds a bachelor's degree in management from Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications (2007). As COO she runs the operations investors are paying for: the fulfilment network (6.0m m² of leased warehouse space across 72 warehouses), 25 customer-service centres, and the operating cadence that took orders from 715m in 2023 to 1.08bn in 2025 while fulfilment cost per order fell from US$18.9 to US$17.7. Her remit also covers the tariff response: the shift to formal customs entry in the US, local inventory in Europe and the trade-compliance function. This is the most important execution task over the next year. Ms Miao beneficially owns 205,579,906 Class A and 471,329,694 Class B shares. The Class B figure includes 393,169,450 shares held by Trendy Group Holdings, a trust whose beneficiaries are the four founders' holding vehicles and whose votes she controls. In total she has about 14.9% of the votes. She sits on the Board's remuneration and nomination committees. Running operations and controlling the founders' shared trust makes her the second most influential person in the Company. Her holdings are also locked up for 24 months.

### Leigh Lei Gui, Chief Financial Officer (age 56)

Mr Gui joined SHEIN as CFO in March 2021 and oversees finance. He handles most of the Company's contact with public-market investors and has more public-company experience than anyone else on the team. Before SHEIN he was CFO of VIPKid (May 2020 to February 2021). From 2005 to 2020 he held finance roles at eBay and PayPal, including CFO of Greater China for eBay and PayPal, CFO of Asia-Pacific cross-border trade at eBay and CFO of international cross-border trade at eBay. That experience is directly relevant, since he has run finance for businesses exposed to customs regimes, currencies, payment platforms and tax in many jurisdictions. He holds a bachelor's degree in genetics from Wuhan University (1990), a master's in molecular biology from Rice University (1995) and an MBA from the University of Toronto (1999). SHEIN's treasury policy under him has been conservative: deposits and certificates of deposit with highly rated banks, no leverage, no speculative trading and no currency hedging. He also built the Hong Kong reporting and non-IFRS framework (adjusted EBIT, adjusted net income) introduced with the Interim Report. Investors will watch his influence on capital allocation most closely. That covers the dividend policy (at least 50% of net profit after significant capex) and the use of US$1.7bn of IPO proceeds: 40% for technology, 40% for brand and global presence, 10% for corporate responsibility and 10% for general purposes. Mr Gui is neither a director nor a WVR beneficiary.

### Gu Xiaoqing, Co-Founder, Executive Director and Chief Product Officer (age 44)

Ms Gu is a co-founder, has been an executive director since the start and has been chief product officer since 2017. She oversees product management: the merchandising, design and category decisions at the centre of LATR. She previously worked with the other founders at the search-engine-marketing firm and holds a bachelor of arts from Nanjing Tech University (2005). Product is where SHEIN's strategy is changing most. The chairman's priority of moving up the price range is mainly a product-portfolio decision, and the Interim Report points to product results: MUSERA's expansion into sportswear; Aloruh's move into swimwear, sleepwear, sportswear and formal dresses; strong growth in childrenswear and sportswear; and the link between the LATR supply chain and the "Design X" AI creative system for partner brands. The Everlane acquisition and the Xcelerator partners (AiiRZ, KIZN, Baby Phat, Fashion SZN) also sit in her product and brand remit. She is also accountable for much of product compliance, which has repeatedly drawn regulatory action in France and the EU: chemical-safety testing (800,000+ apparel inspections in 2025), IP screening (multi-layer image-recognition review) and marketplace listing controls. She beneficially owns 205,579,906 Class A and 78,160,244 Class B shares (about 12.6% of votes), locked up for 24 months.

### Other executives

Ren Xiaoqing (age 40), co-founder and executive director, was chief supply chain officer from 2017 and now oversees public affairs and corporate responsibility. The move reflects how much SHEIN's regulatory and ESG exposure now matters. He owns 205,579,906 Class A and 78,160,244 Class B shares (about 12.6% of votes).

Basten Hao Xu (age 38) has been Chief Technology Officer since the start. He is responsible for technology development, including the 1,700+ in-house software systems that run LATR, the supplier cloud tools and warehouse management. He holds a computer-science degree from Nanjing Agricultural University (2010).

### Board, governance and ownership

- Board: seven directors, the four co-founders plus three independent non-executive directors (INEDs). Denny Lee is a former NetEase CFO and has chaired audit committees at NIO, New Oriental and Jianpu. Hongbin Cai is Dean of HKU Business School and a former Dean of Peking University's Guanghua School. Lim Ming Yan is Chairman of Changi Airport Group and a former Group CEO of CapitaLand. As Hong Kong requires for WVR issuers, the audit and corporate-governance committees consist entirely of INEDs.
- Voting control: only the founders hold Class A shares (ten votes each). On reserved matters, such as changes to constitutional documents, appointment or removal of INEDs and auditors, and winding up, all shares vote one-for-one.
- Economic ownership after the IPO (4,246.2m shares): the founders hold about 2,531m shares (about 59.6%, including Trendy Group). IDG holds about 7.2%, Sequoia China/HongShan-related entities about 3.8%, Sequoia Capital about 5.3% and Greenwoods about 3.9%. The IPO created a public float of about 6.6%.
- Lock-ups: 24 months for the founders, and six months (to about 1 March 2027) for pre-IPO and cornerstone investors. Pre-IPO investors from Series A-C, holding about 34% of the shares, bought at US$0.01-1.28 per share against HK$30.84 (about US$3.93) today, so they may sell heavily when the lock-up ends.
- Remuneration: directors' aggregate pay was about US$3m a year in 2023-25. The five highest-paid individuals (none of them directors in 2025) received US$14m in 2025.
- Compliance adviser: Somerley Capital (permanent, as required for WVR issuers). Auditor: Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu.

Governance is the weakest part of the equity story. The founders hold near-absolute control, the CEO is also chairman, management apart from the CFO has little public-market experience, and the pre-IPO restructuring paid about US$3.5bn of cash (the US$2.2bn Conversion Adjustment plus about US$1.3bn of distributions) to preferred holders. That cash would otherwise have belonged to all shareholders. On the other side are an experienced INED slate, a stated payout policy of at least 50%, and the founders' 24-month lock-up, which ties their horizon to that of minority holders.

## 4. Products and services

### 4.1 First-party brand portfolio (88.7% of 2025 net revenues)

SHEIN's first-party business sells company brands and partnership brands made by contract manufacturers. The flagship SHEIN brand sells value women's fashion. Sub-brands extend the range: MOTF (premium), Glowmode (activewear), SHEGLAM (cosmetics), MUSERA (dresses, now sportswear) and Aloruh (women's fashion, expanding into swimwear, sleepwear and formal wear). Everlane, acquired in 2Q26, adds a US premium-basics brand with a sustainability positioning and an established US customer base. Categories span apparel (63.8% of 2025 revenues), footwear, accessories, beauty, home and living, childrenswear, sportswear and lifestyle. Apparel's share has fallen from 68.8% in 2023 as SHEIN moved into categories that raise order frequency and basket size, though some of them, such as home and living, carry lower margins.

Prices are set at cost plus a margin and adjusted dynamically. Management's stated plan is to keep the value core while adding higher-priced brands. That should lift the platform's average selling price and, because fulfilment costs about US$18 per order regardless of price, improve margins.

### 4.2 LATR: the operating system

LATR is the core of SHEIN's product offer. It has five steps:
1. Data-informed initial batches of 100-200 units per style.
2. Customer orders.
3. Real-time sales data and automated reorders, with orders allocated automatically to suppliers with spare capacity.
4. Restocking in as few as five days.
5. Traffic optimisation to put the best sellers in front of customers.

The economics are attractive: little upfront commitment per style, unsold inventory in the low single digits, and write-downs of about 1-2% of cost of sales, against industry inventory waste that CIC estimates can reach 30%. The chairman acknowledges the model's limit: it works best in categories "driven by diversity, where demand changes rapidly".

### 4.3 Marketplace (service revenues; 11.3% of 2025 net revenues, 13.4% in 1H26)

The marketplace, launched in 2H22 and formally in 2023, lets third-party merchants and brands sell to SHEIN's customers. SHEIN acts as agent and books service revenue: a commission of generally 10-20% of transaction value, plus fulfilment fees for warehousing and delivery. Service revenues grew from US$0.9bn in 2023 to US$4.7bn in 2025, then rose 24.9% in 1H26 (44.2% in 2Q26) as tariff changes pushed more volume through merchants with local inventory. Management calls the recent mix shift a "short-term effect" of trade-policy changes and says first-party remains the core. Because the marketplace has no cost of sales, reported gross margin rose from 60.2% (2023) to 67.9% (2025) and about 70% in 1H26. Only the take rate is booked as revenue, though, so the marketplace raises fulfilment and marketing costs as a percentage of revenue. SHEIN does not disclose GMV. At our assumed 25% blended take rate, 2025 marketplace GMV would be about US$19bn, and total gross sales (first-party revenue plus marketplace GMV) about US$56bn.

### 4.4 Brand enablement: SHEIN Xcelerator

The Xcelerator programme, upgraded from SHEIN X in October 2025, opens SHEIN's supply chain, design tools (including the Design X AI system), marketing and fulfilment to partner brands, from start-ups to established labels. The Prospectus cites one partner whose sales grew about 15x in its second year while its operating margin improved by 30+pp and its inventory days fell by two-thirds. It also says brand-enablement operating margin is roughly twice the group's. The business is small, no more than 1% of revenue in 2025, but it is SHEIN's clearest route to higher-margin, capital-light "infrastructure" revenue. Nearly 20 brands have joined.

### 4.5 Circularity and other services

SHEIN Exchange, a peer-to-peer resale platform for items bought from or through SHEIN in the US, France, the UK and Germany, had 9.5 million active users at the end of 2025. It supports SHEIN's sustainability case but is not financially material.

### 4.6 Supply chain and fulfilment infrastructure

- Suppliers: about 7,500 contract manufacturers in 2025 (5,800 in 2023), with no exclusivity, one-year renewable contracts and no minimum purchase commitments. Free cloud software manages orders, production, quality control and shipping. SHEIN ran 5,150 on-site social-responsibility (SRS) audits in 2025 (more than 99% by third-party auditors), covering suppliers that represent about 95% of company-brand procurement value.
- Warehouses: 6.0m m² leased at 30 June 2026, made up of 37 in China (4.1m m²), 6 in the US (0.4m m²), 18 in Europe (1.0m m²) and 11 elsewhere (0.5m m²). More than 90% of 2025 revenue shipped from central warehouses in mainland China, though local inventory is growing, especially in Europe.
- Delivery: third-party logistics; more than 90% of orders delivered within 10 business days of dispatch; long-term air-freight capacity agreements.
- Technology: 1,700+ proprietary software systems; data hosted on AWS (EU data in Spain) and Microsoft Azure (US data in the US); ISO 27001/27701 and PCI DSS certification; a cybersecurity team of about 190 people. R&D spending was US$451m in 1H26.

## 5. Customers and go-to-market

**Customer base.** SHEIN served about 273 million active customers in 2025 (291 million in the twelve months to June 2026), mainly young women. Order frequency has held at about four orders per customer a year (3.8 in 2023, 4.0 in 2024-25, 3.9 for the twelve months to March 2026), so growth has come from new customers, with spending per customer roughly flat. Net revenue per active customer fell from about US$173 in 2023 to about US$153 in 2025 because of marketplace mix, faster growth in markets with smaller baskets, and category mix.

**Geographic mix.** Growth has moved sharply away from the US. US revenue fell 3.5% in 2025 and 9.9% in 1H26. Europe grew 8.8% in 2025, then fell 13.9% in 2Q26 ahead of the EU duty change. The rest of the world grew 15.4% in 2025 and 21.6% in 2Q26, led by Latin America, and made up 44% of revenue in 1H26 against 39% in 2023.

**Channels and marketing.** SHEIN sells almost entirely through its own apps and websites, which have local-language versions, personalised recommendations and region-specific layouts, plus pop-ups, showrooms and events. Marketing is mostly digital: influencer partnerships (a practice SHEIN pioneered at scale), paid search, product-listing ads, paid social and SEO. Marketing spend rose from 10.7% of revenue in 2024 to 14.8% in 2025 (advertising rose from US$4.0bn to US$5.0bn) as SHEIN competed with Temu for customers' attention while raising prices to cover tariffs. It fell to 13.6% in 2Q26 when SHEIN cut European advertising ahead of the duty change. SHEIN plans to spend 40% of the IPO proceeds (about HK$5.3bn) on brand-building and performance marketing over 48 months.

**Customer terms.** Customers pay when they order. Shipping is free above set thresholds, returns are generous (free return shipping in selected regions), and refunds may be issued without a return. Prepayment drives the negative working-capital cycle, with turnover days for net revenues of –12 to –13.

**Seasonality.** The fourth quarter, with 11.11, Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas, is the largest promotional window. Working capital builds in the first half and unwinds in the second: operating cash flow was US$2.9bn in 1H25 and –US$39m in 2H25.

## 6. Industry overview

**Market size.** CIC sizes the global fashion industry (apparel, footwear and accessories, retail sales value excluding VAT) at US$1,737bn in 2025. It grew at a 2.7% CAGR over 2021-25 and CIC forecasts 3.4% growth to US$2,049bn by 2030. Apparel is US$1,370bn and footwear US$367bn. The online channel was US$606bn in 2025 (34.9% penetration) and is forecast to reach US$792bn by 2030 (38.7% penetration). That is a 5.5% CAGR, roughly twice offline growth (2.1%).

**Structure.** The industry is highly fragmented: the top 20 players held only about 20.5% of the market in 2025. CIC attributes this to the "trilemma": few players can offer broad assortment, rapid design refresh and efficient inventory at the same time, so most stay in narrow niches.

**Trends.**
1. Shopping continues to move online, helped by mobile payments, global fulfilment, social commerce and recommendation technology.
2. Social media speeds up trend cycles and splits demand into a long tail, which favours broad assortments that refresh quickly.
3. Consumers and regulators look harder at labour practices, environmental impact and transparency. The EU is legislating on textile waste, product safety and platform accountability.
4. Small-batch production, real-time inventory management and warehouse automation are becoming standard.
5. Governments are re-regulating cross-border e-commerce. The US ended de minimis for China (May 2025) and for all countries (August 2025). The EU abolished the €150 duty exemption from 1 July 2026, with a flat €3 duty per item category and a handling fee to follow, and other markets are reviewing similar thresholds. These changes permanently raise the landed cost of cross-border parcels sent direct to consumers relative to locally stocked inventory.

**Adjacent categories.** CIC sizes home and living at US$1,408bn (online US$282bn, rising to US$374bn by 2030), appliances and electronics at US$1,441bn (online US$659bn to US$1,067bn) and beauty and personal care at US$614bn (online US$171bn to US$264bn). These markets support SHEIN's category expansion and marketplace strategy.

**Input costs.** Polyester made up 82.7% of SHEIN's raw material by weight in 2025. China's man-made-fibre price index fell from 104.2 (2022) to 94.0 (2025), and China's apparel and footwear PPI was broadly flat (102.0 in 2025 against 100.0 in 2021). Input costs have therefore been benign; freight and duties are the variable costs that matter more.

## 7. Competition

SHEIN competes mainly with global fashion retailers and, to a lesser extent, with general e-commerce platforms that sell fashion.

**1. Inditex (Zara), the benchmark fast-fashion operator.** Market share of about 2.5% (CIC). Trailing revenue of about €41bn, an EBITDA margin of about 24% and a net margin of about 16%; it trades at about 23.5x forward P/E. Inditex sources close to home (Spain, Portugal, Morocco, Turkey), which gives it two-to-four-week design-to-shelf cycles across stores and online. SHEIN offers a far larger assortment at lower prices. Inditex has stronger brand equity, a better reputation for quality, physical stores, and much higher margins and returns.

**2. H&M Group.** Revenue of about €19bn equivalent (SEK221bn), an EBITDA margin of about 11% and a forward P/E of about 19x. Longer lead times and a store-heavy model leave H&M more exposed to markdowns, and it has been losing share in value fashion to SHEIN and Primark.

**3. Fast Retailing (Uniqlo).** Market share of about 1.3% and revenue of ¥3.85trn. Its premium valuation (about 39x forward P/E and 22.6x EV/EBITDA) reflects a basics-led model with little fashion risk and strong growth in Asia. It overlaps little with SHEIN's trend-led assortment but shows what a global apparel franchise can be worth.

**4. Athletic leaders (Nike, Adidas).** Ranked first and fourth by CIC market share. They matter as share benchmarks and as competitors in sportswear, where SHEIN's MUSERA and Glowmode are growing.

**5. PDD Holdings (Temu).** SHEIN's most direct and aggressive competitor since 2023. Temu is a fully managed and semi-managed marketplace that ships low-priced Chinese goods worldwide, backed by PDD's net cash of about US$66bn. It competes on price in every category, including fashion, and has fought SHEIN in court (a US antitrust complaint, partially dismissed in September 2025; UK copyright and competition cases, with further trials in 2027). Temu faces the same de minimis changes. PDD trades at only about 6.8x forward P/E and about 3x EV/EBITDA, which anchors what investors expect to pay for China-linked cross-border platforms.

**6. Alibaba (AliExpress) and other Chinese cross-border platforms.** AliExpress's "Choice" programme competes on price and delivery. Alibaba trades at about 13.6x forward P/E.

**7. Amazon (including Amazon Haul).** Amazon's low-price storefront targets the same value shopper and has local fulfilment in the US and Europe. As duties erode the cost advantage of shipping direct from China, Amazon's logistics scale becomes a bigger threat.

**8. TikTok Shop.** Social-commerce platforms compete for the same young consumers and the same influencer budgets, and they have made it cheaper for small fashion brands to acquire customers.

**9. Online fashion specialists: Zalando, ASOS, Boohoo, Revolve.** Zalando (revenue of about €13.5bn; about 12.9x forward P/E) is the leading European online fashion platform and a direct competitor in Europe; it is expanding through its acquisition of About You. ASOS (loss-making, EV/Sales of about 0.5x) and Boohoo have lost share to SHEIN in UK and US value fashion. Revolve (about 21x forward P/E) is a US premium online retailer.

**10. Primark (ABF) and off-price retailers.** Physical value fashion with strong unit economics in Europe. They gain from any rise in cross-border landed costs.

**SHEIN's competitive position.** SHEIN's advantages are assortment breadth (2m+ styles), speed (5-day restocks), inventory efficiency (36-day turnover), low costs (value pricing made possible by LATR and the density of the Chinese supply chain) and a large, engaged customer base (291m). Its weaknesses are thin operating margins (2-4%, against 12-20% for Inditex and Fast Retailing); reliance on shipping from China (more than 90% of revenue ships from Chinese warehouses) just as duty regimes tighten; brand perception problems around quality, sustainability and IP; and regulatory scrutiny that physical-retail peers largely avoid. As duties rise, the main competitive threat is moving away from Temu, which competes on price, toward platforms with local fulfilment such as Amazon and toward local value retailers.

## 8. Market opportunity (TAM)

We look at SHEIN's addressable market in three layers:

| Layer | 2025 size | 2030E size | CAGR | SHEIN 2025 revenue / share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global fashion (apparel, footwear, accessories) | US$1,737bn | US$2,049bn | 3.4% | ~1.9% share (CIC, by RSV) |
| Global online fashion | US$606bn | US$792bn | 5.5% | Largest online player |
| Online home & living | US$282bn | US$374bn | 5.8% | Adjacent category |
| Online beauty & personal care | US$171bn | US$264bn | 9.1% | SHEGLAM + marketplace |
| Online appliances & electronics | US$659bn | US$1,067bn | 10.1% | Marketplace only |

*Source: CIC via Prospectus. RSV = retail sales value excluding VAT.*

**Serviceable market.** SHEIN's core serviceable market is online fashion (US$606bn in 2025). Its apparel revenue of about US$27bn, plus part of its footwear and accessories revenue, implies a share in the high single digits. Adding online beauty and home and living takes the serviceable online market to about US$1.06trn in 2025, rising to about US$1.43trn by 2030 (6.2% CAGR).

**Room to gain share.** After a decade of very fast growth, SHEIN still holds under 2% of global fashion, a share management calls "relatively low". Our base case has net revenues growing at a 6.1% CAGR over 2026-31E to US$56bn, slightly faster than the online fashion market. Orders grow 5-7% a year, and revenue per order stabilises as the move to higher price points offsets marketplace mix. Our bull case (8.6% CAGR) assumes Europe re-accelerates after the duty shock and the rest of the world keeps growing at double-digit rates. Our bear case (2.8% CAGR) assumes a structural loss of share in the US and Europe.

**Constraints.** Trade policy and regulation limit SHEIN's growth more than market size does. The US and EU, SHEIN's two largest regions and about 60% of 2025 revenue, have removed their low-value exemptions. That raises the cost of the direct-from-China model and pushes SHEIN toward local inventory, which delivers faster but carries higher working capital, warehousing and duty costs.

## 9. Risk assessment

### Company-specific risks

1. **Trade-policy and duty risk (High).** More than 90% of 2025 revenue shipped from Chinese warehouses. The US ended de minimis in 2025, and tariff rates on China-origin goods are now 10-87.5%, up from 0-62.5%. The EU abolished its €150 exemption from July 2026 and will add a handling fee. SHEIN warns that EU trends could be "in line with or exceed" the US experience, where revenue fell 9.9% in 1H26. Duties are booked in fulfilment expenses. Further escalation, such as the China-US tariff truce expiring on 10 November 2026, would hit margins directly.
2. **Regulatory and legal risk (High).** Open matters include an EU Digital Services Act formal investigation (opened February 2026; DSA fines can reach 6% of global turnover); an FTC investigation of US operations that could lead to a "significant" settlement; a €150m CNIL fine under appeal; €22.5m of DGCCRF fines (June 2026) that SHEIN is contesting; an Irish DPC inquiry into EU-to-China data transfers (April 2026); and litigation with Temu in the US and UK. Provisions were about US$80m at March 2026, plus the CNIL provision. We deduct a US$1.0bn contingency reserve in our valuation.
3. **Margin-recovery risk (High).** Operating margin fell to 2.4% in 1H26 (2.1% in 2Q26) from 5.2% in 1H25 as fulfilment costs rose to 50.4% of revenue. Our base case has margins recovering to about 3.0% in 2027 and about 4.6% by 2031. If SHEIN cannot pass on duties, or freight stays high, or local inventory costs more than expected, margins could stay near 2%.
4. **Labour, supply-chain and ESG risk (Medium-High).** SHEIN relies on thousands of small suppliers and their subcontractors, and audits cannot catch every violation. Allegations of labour abuse, forced labour in upstream cotton or chemical-safety failures could lead to import restrictions (for example under US forced-labour law), consumer boycotts or exclusion from institutional ESG portfolios.
5. **Brand, IP and product-safety risk (Medium).** SHEIN regularly faces IP-infringement claims and product-safety findings, particularly on the marketplace; French authorities pursued SHEIN in 2025 over inappropriate marketplace products. Bad publicity can erode customer trust.
6. **Governance and capital-allocation risk (Medium).** The WVR structure gives the founders 83% of the votes, the CEO also chairs the Board, and the pre-IPO arrangements paid about US$3.5bn of cash to preferred holders. Minority shareholders have little recourse if cash goes into low-return acquisitions (SPARC lost money; Everlane is small).

### Industry and market risks

7. **Competition (High).** Temu, AliExpress, TikTok Shop and Amazon Haul compete for the same value customer. Heavy subsidies by better-funded platforms could force SHEIN to spend more on marketing, which was already 14.8% of revenue in 2025.
8. **Fashion and consumer-demand risk (Medium).** SHEIN's customers are young and price-sensitive, so a consumer slowdown or inflation shock in its main markets would hit order volumes. Management expects the "challenging global consumer environment" to persist in the near term.
9. **Channel and platform-dependence risk (Medium).** Customer acquisition depends on Google, Meta, TikTok and the app stores. Changes in algorithms, privacy rules (such as cookie-consent enforcement) or ad pricing could raise acquisition costs.

### Financial risks

10. **Freight, oil and currency risk (Medium).** The Iran conflict raised oil prices and freight costs in 1H26 and disrupted demand in the Middle East, cutting 2026 revenue growth by a low single-digit number of percentage points. SHEIN does not hedge currency exposure; exchange losses were US$151m in 1H26.
11. **Share-overhang and liquidity risk (High, near term).** The IPO float was only 6.6% of shares, and much of it went to existing holders; stabilisation bought back another 15% of the offer. The six-month lock-up for pre-IPO investors (about 34% of shares, many with very low cost bases) and cornerstones ends around 1 March 2027, and selling then could weigh on the share price.
12. **Tax risk (Medium).** The OECD Pillar Two minimum tax, transfer-pricing scrutiny and possible challenges to tax residency could raise the effective tax rate (core ETR of about 26% in 1H26, against about 21% in 2025).

### Macroeconomic risks

13. **Geopolitical risk (High).** US-China tensions, sanctions or data-security actions, as other China-linked apps have faced, could restrict SHEIN's business in the US, which is still 22% of revenue.
14. **Recession and interest-rate risk (Medium).** A recession would cut discretionary spending. Lower interest rates would reduce interest income, which is about 27% of 2027E adjusted pre-tax profit in our model.
