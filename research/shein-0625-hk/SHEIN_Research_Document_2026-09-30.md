## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

SHEIN Global Holdings Ltd ("SHEIN", "the Company") is the world's largest online fashion destination by apparel and footwear retail sales value and, according to the industry consultant engaged for the IPO (China Insights Consultancy, "CIC"), the third-largest apparel and footwear company globally with a 1.9% share of a US$1.74trn market in 2025 — behind only a US athletic-wear leader (3.0%) and a Spanish fast-fashion group (2.5%), and ahead of a German athletic brand (1.7%) and a Japanese casual-wear retailer (1.3%). In 2025 SHEIN generated net revenues of **US$41.8bn** (+8.0% YoY), operating income of **US$1.71bn** (4.1% margin) and IFRS net income of **US$2.06bn**, serving approximately **273 million active customers** across roughly **160 markets** and fulfilling **1.08 billion orders**. Net revenues have compounded at 14.2% p.a. over 2023-25 and active customers at 21.2% p.a.

The business model is best described as a **vertically integrated, first-party-led online fashion brand collection** with a growing marketplace attached. SHEIN designs (with 370+ in-house designers and a network of contract manufacturers and independent designers), commissions production from more than **7,500 contract manufacturers** (predominantly SMEs in the Pearl River Delta), stores the output mostly in central warehouses in mainland China, and ships it directly to consumers through its own apps and websites. It does not operate physical stores at scale. Revenue is recognised on delivery; the substantial majority of customers pay in advance, which, combined with ~34-day supplier terms and ~36-day inventory turnover, produces a structurally **negative working capital** position (–US$5.0bn at 30 June 2026) and a large cash pile.

The Company's core intellectual property is an operating model it calls **LATR — Large-scale Automated Test and Reorder**. New styles are launched in initial batches of roughly 100-200 units, customer response is measured in real time, and winners are reordered — restocking in as few as five days — while losers are dropped. SHEIN argues LATR resolves the fashion industry's "trilemma" of assortment breadth, design-refresh speed and inventory efficiency. The numbers support the claim: more than **2 million apparel styles** were live at 31 March 2026, customers could discover ~**4,700 new apparel styles per day** in 1Q26, inventory turnover was **36 days** in 2025 and inventory write-downs have run at only ~1-2% of cost of sales.

Revenue is generated in two ways. **Product revenues** (US$37.1bn in 2025, 88.7% of the total) come from first-party sales of company brands (SHEIN, MOTF, Glowmode, SHEGLAM, MUSERA, Aloruh and others) and partnership brands. **Service revenues** (US$4.7bn, 11.3%) are commissions (generally 10-20% of transaction value) and fulfilment fees charged to third-party merchants on the marketplace launched in 2022-23, plus fees from brand-enablement partners. Because service revenue is recognised net, the rising marketplace mix depresses reported revenue per order and inflates fulfilment costs as a percentage of revenue — a key analytical nuance for the stock.

Geographically SHEIN is diversified: in 2025 the **United States** contributed 24.1% of net revenues (US$10.1bn), **Europe** 35.4% (US$14.8bn) and the **rest of the world** 40.5% (US$16.9bn; led by Latin America and the Middle East). By category, apparel is 63.8% and "others" (footwear, accessories, beauty, home and lifestyle) 36.2%. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue and the top five suppliers account for ~16% of purchases.

SHEIN is headquartered in Singapore (since late 2021), incorporated in the Cayman Islands, and conducts operations through subsidiaries in China, Singapore, the US, Ireland, the UK and the UAE. It employed 17,585 people at 30 June 2026, ~79% of them in mainland China. The Company listed in Hong Kong on 1 September 2026 under a weighted-voting-rights (WVR) structure, raising HK$13.6bn gross (HK$13.2bn net) by selling 279,992,500 new Class B shares at HK$48.56, valuing it at ~US$26bn — roughly a quarter of the ~US$100bn private valuation reached in 2022. The shares have since fallen to HK$30.84 (–36.5%), taking the fully diluted market capitalisation to ~US$17.4bn.

**The current investment debate** centres on four issues: (1) the removal of de minimis/low-value import exemptions in the US (May/August 2025) and the EU (1 July 2026), which raises landed costs on the direct-from-China model; (2) margin compression in 2026 — operating margin fell to 2.4% in 1H26 from 5.2% in 1H25 as freight rates spiked during the Iran conflict and the Company chose to absorb costs; (3) regulatory exposure in the EU and US (EU Digital Services Act investigation, FTC investigation, French and Irish data/consumer proceedings); and (4) governance and capital allocation, following ~US$3.5bn of cash paid to preferred shareholders around the listing. Against this, SHEIN has **US$13.5bn of pro forma unrestricted cash** (~78% of its market value; US$13.9bn including investments), still-growing order volumes (+6.4% in 1H26) and customers (+14.6% LTM to 291m), and a dividend policy targeting at least 50% of annual net profit.

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

**Origins (2008-2014).** Press accounts trace SHEIN's roots to a cross-border e-commerce business started in Nanjing in 2008 by Xu Yangtian, a search-engine-marketing specialist who sold apparel to overseas buyers online. The Prospectus dates the business's formal launch to **2012**, when Mr Xu and his three co-founders — Miao Miao, Gu Xiaoqing and Ren Xiaoqing, all former colleagues at a company providing search-engine marketing services to Chinese exporters — started the venture. The founders' background matters: SHEIN was built by digital marketers rather than by merchants or designers, and its early edge came from performance marketing, social-media and influencer engagement and data-driven merchandising. The **SHEIN** brand was introduced in 2013, and the Cayman holding company (then named Elite Depot Limited) was incorporated on 12 May 2014.

**Building the machine (2014-2019).** In 2014 SHEIN pilot-tested LATR, and in 2015 it debuted its mobile app. During this period the Company built its operational base in Guangzhou, embedding itself in the dense garment-manufacturing cluster of the Pearl River Delta and progressively digitising its suppliers through free cloud-based production-management software. Financing followed the model's traction: a Series A in 2015 (US$5m at a US$53m pre-money valuation), a Series B from 2016 (RMB169m at RMB1.1bn), and a Series C in 2018-19 (US$243m at US$2.4bn). Annual active customers reached 10 million in 2018.

**Hyper-growth (2020-2022).** The pandemic shift to online shopping, combined with SHEIN's TikTok- and Instagram-native marketing, turned the Company into a global phenomenon. Active customers passed 50 million in 2020 and 100 million in 2021, the year SHEIN launched the **SHEIN X** designer-incubation programme and moved its headquarters to Singapore. A Series C+ in March 2020 valued the Company at US$5bn; a Series D in 2022 raised US$1.8bn at a **US$98.2bn** pre-money valuation (US$23.72 per share, split-adjusted). Net revenues exceeded US$20bn in 2022. This was the valuation peak.

**Marketplace, scrutiny and re-rating (2023-2025).** In 2023 SHEIN officially launched its marketplace for third-party merchants and raised US$1.7bn in a Series D+ at a **US$64.0bn** pre-money valuation — a 35% down-round. It also took a roughly one-third stake in SPARC Group (then operator of brands including Forever 21) in exchange for Class B shares, a partnership intended to combine SPARC's physical retail with SHEIN's on-demand supply chain; the investment has since produced cumulative associate losses. Temu's aggressive entry into Western markets in 2023 triggered litigation in both directions (the US antitrust complaint by Temu, and SHEIN's UK copyright claims). Revenue grew 41.1% in 2023 and 20.7% in 2024; active customers passed 200 million in 2024. A planned New York listing stalled amid US political scrutiny, as did a London listing attempt; the Company ultimately filed in Hong Kong. In 2025 growth slowed to 8.0% as the US removed the de minimis exemption for Chinese imports (effective 2 May 2025) and then for all countries (29 August 2025); US revenue fell 3.5%. In October 2025 SHEIN upgraded SHEIN X into the **SHEIN Xcelerator Programme**, a brand-enablement platform. Regulatory costs rose: a €40m DGCCRF settlement in France (July 2025) and a €150m CNIL cookie-consent fine (September 2025, under appeal).

**Road to listing (2026).** In March 2026 the preferred shareholders' redemption date was extended from 4 March to 31 December 2026 in exchange for ~US$1.1bn of 8% p.a. distributions (paid March-September 2026), a 12% p.a. coupon from 5 March to listing (~US$230m), and a full-ratchet style "Conversion Adjustment" for Series Pre-D, D and D+ holders, settled at listing by **US$2.2bn of cash** plus 19.6 million additional shares. The EU approved the end of the €150 customs-duty exemption in February 2026 (effective 1 July 2026, with a €3 flat duty per item category). In the second quarter SHEIN acquired **Everlane**, a US premium basics brand, for US$78m (mostly repayment of Everlane debt). The Hong Kong IPO priced in late August 2026 at HK$48.56, near the mid-point of the HK$47.60-49.50 range; the retail tranche was 5.63x covered and the international book 2.59x, with seven cornerstone investors — mostly existing shareholders (Boyu, Tiger Global, General Atlantic, a Tencent affiliate, Greenwoods) plus Taikang Life and UBS AM Singapore — taking 22.1% of the offer. The shares opened flat, fell as low as HK$43.72 on day one, and slid through September; the stabilising manager bought back the full 15% over-allocation at HK$35.90-48.56 and the over-allotment option **lapsed** on 26 September. On 28 September SHEIN reported its first interim results (1H26 revenue +1.0%, operating income –52.9%); the stock fell 10.7% the next day and closed at a record low of HK$30.84 on 30 September.

## 3. MANAGEMENT TEAM AND GOVERNANCE

SHEIN remains founder-led and founder-controlled. All four co-founders sit on the seven-member Board as executive directors, and together they control **83.3% of the votes** on ordinary resolutions through Class A shares carrying ten votes each. Two professional executives — the CFO and CTO — complete the senior management team disclosed in the Prospectus.

### Xu Yangtian — Founder, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (age 43)

Mr Xu founded SHEIN and has served as chairman, executive director and CEO since inception; he is responsible for group strategy and business management. Before SHEIN he worked with his co-founders at a company providing search-engine marketing to Chinese export businesses, and he holds a bachelor's degree in international trade from Qingdao University of Technology (2007). His background explains much of SHEIN's DNA: an obsession with customer-acquisition efficiency, rapid A/B testing and data-driven decision-making that was later applied to merchandising through LATR. Mr Xu is famously low-profile — he has given almost no interviews and the first Chairman's Statement in the Interim Report is one of his few public written communications. That statement is revealing: he frames SHEIN as a "richly diversified brand collection" built on a "vertically integrated, first-party-led" model, commits that the first-party business "will always remain at the core", and sets three one-to-two-year priorities — broadening the price range towards higher-priced brands, raising quality and compliance investment, and more proactive consumer communication/brand marketing. He also defended the decision to absorb elevated freight costs in 2Q26 rather than raise prices, calling them transitory. After the IPO, Mr Xu beneficially owns 798,973,148 Class A and 487,090,102 Class B shares — **30.3% of the share capital and 49.9% of the votes** — through Apex Sight Holdings, which he controls via a family trust. As the largest WVR beneficiary and the controlling shareholder, he effectively determines board composition and strategic direction. His holdings are subject to a **24-month lock-up**. For investors, Mr Xu's record is one of exceptional execution in building scale (net revenues above US$40bn within roughly 13 years of the 2012 launch) but also of a reserved, control-oriented style; the combination of CEO and chairman roles is a disclosed deviation from Hong Kong's Corporate Governance Code.

### Miao Miao — Co-Founder, Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer (age 41)

Ms Miao is a co-founder, has been an executive director since inception and chief operating officer since 2017, overseeing the Group's day-to-day operations. She also serves as a director or senior manager of several subsidiaries. Like Mr Xu she previously worked at the search-engine-marketing firm where the founders met, and she holds a bachelor's degree in management from Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications (2007). As COO she sits at the centre of the operational machinery that investors are underwriting: the fulfilment network (6.0m m² of leased warehouse space across 72 warehouses), customer service (25 centres) and the operating cadence that turned 715m orders in 2023 into 1.08bn in 2025 while cutting fulfilment cost per order from US$18.9 to US$17.7. Her operational remit also covers the tariff response — shifting to formal customs entry in the US, localising inventory in Europe and running the trade-compliance function — which is the single most important near-term execution task. Ms Miao beneficially owns 205,579,906 Class A and 471,329,694 Class B shares (the latter including 393,169,450 shares held by Trendy Group Holdings, a trust whose beneficiaries are the four founders' holding vehicles and whose votes she controls), giving her **~14.9% of the votes**. She sits on the Board's remuneration and nomination committees. Her dual role as operator and controller of the founders' shared trust makes her the second most influential person in the Company; her holdings are also locked up for 24 months.

### Leigh Lei Gui — Chief Financial Officer (age 56)

Mr Gui joined SHEIN as CFO in March 2021 and oversees finance. He is the Company's most important interface with public-market investors and the most experienced public-company executive on the team. Before SHEIN he was CFO of VIPKid (May 2020 to February 2021), and from 2005 to 2020 he held a series of finance roles at eBay and PayPal, including CFO of Greater China at eBay and PayPal, CFO of Asia-Pacific cross-border trade at eBay and CFO of international cross-border trade at eBay. That cross-border e-commerce background is directly relevant: he has managed the finance function for businesses exposed to customs regimes, FX, payment platforms and multi-jurisdiction tax. He holds a bachelor's degree in genetics from Wuhan University (1990), a master's in molecular biology from Rice University (1995) and an MBA from the University of Toronto (1999). Under his watch SHEIN has run a conservative treasury policy — deposits and certificates of deposit with highly rated banks, no leverage, no speculative trading, no FX hedging — and has built the Hong Kong reporting and non-IFRS framework (adjusted EBIT, adjusted net income) introduced with the Interim Report. Capital allocation is the area where his influence will be most scrutinised: the dividend policy (≥50% of net profit after significant capex) and the deployment of US$1.7bn of IPO proceeds (40% technology, 40% brand and global presence, 10% corporate responsibility, 10% general purposes). Mr Gui is not a director and is not a WVR beneficiary.

### Gu Xiaoqing — Co-Founder, Executive Director and Chief Product Officer (age 44)

Ms Gu is a co-founder, has been an executive director since inception and chief product officer since 2017, overseeing product management — the merchandising, design and category decisions that sit at the heart of LATR. She previously worked with the other founders at the search-engine-marketing firm and holds a bachelor of arts from Nanjing Tech University (2005). Product is where SHEIN's strategy is changing most visibly. The chairman's stated priority of moving up the price range is essentially a product-portfolio decision, and the Interim Report highlights product-led wins: MUSERA's expansion into sportswear, Aloruh's push into swimwear, sleepwear, sportswear and formal dresses, strong growth in childrenswear and sportswear, and the integration of the LATR supply chain with the "Design X" AI creative system for partner brands. The Everlane acquisition and the Xcelerator partners (AiiRZ, KIZN, Baby Phat, Fashion SZN) also fall within the product and brand agenda. Ms Gu will also carry much of the accountability for product-compliance issues — chemical-safety testing (800,000+ apparel inspections in 2025), IP screening (multi-layer image-recognition review) and marketplace listing controls — which have been a recurring source of regulatory action in France and the EU. She beneficially owns 205,579,906 Class A and 78,160,244 Class B shares (~12.6% of votes), locked up for 24 months.

He owns 205,579,906 Class A and 78,160,244 Class B shares (~12.6% of votes).

### Board, governance and ownership

- **Board:** seven directors — the four co-founders plus three independent non-executive directors: **Denny Lee** (former NetEase CFO; audit-committee chair at NIO, New Oriental and Jianpu), **Hongbin Cai** (Dean of HKU Business School; former Dean of Peking University's Guanghua School) and **Lim Ming Yan** (Chairman of Changi Airport Group; former Group CEO of CapitaLand). Audit and corporate-governance committees are composed entirely of INEDs, as required for WVR issuers.

- **Economic ownership after IPO (4,246.2m shares):** founders ~2,531m shares (~59.6%, including Trendy Group); IDG ~7.2%, Sequoia China/HongShan-related ~3.8% of total, Sequoia Capital ~5.3%, Greenwoods ~3.9%; public float ~6.6% from the IPO.
- **Lock-ups:** founders 24 months; pre-IPO investors and cornerstone investors **six months (to ~1 March 2027)**. Pre-IPO investors (~34% of shares) from Series A-C have deeply in-the-money positions (cost of US$0.01-1.28 per share vs. HK$30.84 ≈ US$3.93), creating a potential supply overhang when the lock-up ends.

**Assessment:** Governance is the weakest part of the equity story. Founders hold near-absolute control, the CEO is also chairman, management has limited public-market experience beyond the CFO, and the pre-IPO restructuring transferred ~US$3.5bn of cash (US$2.2bn Conversion Adjustment + ~US$1.3bn distributions) to preferred holders — cash that would otherwise belong to all shareholders. Offsetting features are an experienced INED slate, a stated ≥50% payout policy and the founders' 24-month lock-up, which aligns their horizon with minority holders.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

### 4.1 First-party brand portfolio (88.7% of 2025 net revenues)

SHEIN's first-party business sells company-branded and partnership-brand products sourced from contract manufacturers. The flagship **SHEIN** brand focuses on value women's fashion; sub-brands extend the range: **MOTF** (premium), **Glowmode** (activewear), **SHEGLAM** (cosmetics), **MUSERA** (dresses, now sportswear) and **Aloruh** (women's fashion expanding into swimwear, sleepwear and formal). **Everlane**, acquired in 2Q26, adds a US-heritage premium-basics brand with a sustainability positioning and an established US customer base. Categories span apparel (63.8% of 2025 revenues), footwear, accessories, beauty, home & living, childrenswear, sportswear and lifestyle. The apparel share has declined from 68.8% in 2023 as SHEIN broadened into categories that raise order frequency and basket size, though some (home & living) carry lower margins.

**Pricing** is cost-plus with dynamic adjustment. Management's stated strategy is to keep the value core while adding higher-priced brands, which should lift the platform's average selling price and — given operating leverage on a per-order fulfilment cost base of ~US$18 — improve margins.

### 4.2 LATR: the operating system

LATR is the heart of the product proposition. Its five steps are: (1) data-informed small initial batches of 100-200 units per style; (2) customer orders; (3) real-time sales insights and automated reorders, with orders auto-allocated to suppliers with spare capacity; (4) restock in as few as five days; and (5) traffic optimisation to surface winners. The economics are compelling: low upfront commitment per style, unsold inventory in the low single digits and write-downs of ~1-2% of cost of sales, compared with industry inventory waste that CIC estimates can reach 30%. The model's limit, as the chairman acknowledges, is that it is best suited to categories "driven by diversity, where demand changes rapidly".

### 4.3 Marketplace (service revenues; 11.3% of 2025 net revenues, 13.4% in 1H26)

Launched in 2H22 and formally in 2023, the marketplace lets third-party merchants and brands sell to SHEIN's customers. SHEIN acts as agent and recognises service revenue: a **commission of generally 10-20%** of transaction value plus **fulfilment fees** for warehousing and delivery. Service revenues grew from US$0.9bn in 2023 to US$4.7bn in 2025 and +24.9% in 1H26 (+44.2% in 2Q26) as tariff changes pushed more volume through local-inventory merchants. Management describes the recent mix shift as a "short-term effect" of trade-policy changes and insists first-party remains the core. The marketplace does not incur cost of sales, which is why reported gross margin rose from 60.2% (2023) to 67.9% (2025) and ~70% in 1H26 — but because only the take rate is booked as revenue, it raises fulfilment and marketing costs as a percentage of revenue. SHEIN does not disclose GMV; at an assumed 25% blended take rate (our estimate), 2025 marketplace GMV would be ~US$19bn and total gross sales (first-party revenue plus marketplace GMV) ~US$56bn.

### 4.4 Brand enablement: SHEIN Xcelerator

The Xcelerator programme (upgraded from SHEIN X in October 2025) opens SHEIN's supply chain, design tools (including the Design X AI system), marketing and fulfilment to partner brands, from start-ups to established labels. The Prospectus cites one partner that grew sales ~15x in its second year while improving operating margin by 30+pp and cutting inventory days by two-thirds, and states that brand-enablement operating margin is roughly **twice the group's**. It is small — no more than 1% of revenue in 2025 — but it is the clearest path to a higher-margin, capital-light "infrastructure" revenue stream. Nearly 20 brands have been enabled.

### 4.5 Circularity and other services

**SHEIN Exchange**, a peer-to-peer resale platform for items bought from or through SHEIN (US, France, UK, Germany), had 9.5 million active users at end-2025. It is strategically important for SHEIN's sustainability narrative rather than financially material.

### 4.6 Supply chain and fulfilment infrastructure

- **Suppliers:** ~7,500 contract manufacturers in 2025 (5,800 in 2023), no exclusivity, one-year renewable contracts, no minimum purchase commitments; free cloud software manages orders, production, QA and shipping. 5,150 on-site social-responsibility (SRS) audits in 2025 (>99% by third-party auditors) covering suppliers representing ~95% of company-branded procurement value.
- **Warehouses:** 6.0m m² leased at 30 June 2026 — 37 in China (4.1m m²), 6 in the US (0.4m m²), 18 in Europe (1.0m m²) and 11 elsewhere (0.5m m²). Over 90% of 2025 revenue shipped from central warehouses in mainland China; localisation (especially in Europe) is increasing.
- **Delivery:** third-party logistics; >90% of orders delivered within 10 business days of dispatch; long-term air-freight capacity agreements.
- **Technology:** 1,700+ proprietary software systems; data hosted on AWS (EU data in Spain) and Microsoft Azure (US data in the US); ISO 27001/27701 and PCI DSS certified; ~190-person cybersecurity organisation. R&D spend was US$451m in 1H26.

## 5. CUSTOMERS & GO-TO-MARKET

**Customer base.** SHEIN served ~273 million active customers in 2025 (291 million in the twelve months to June 2026), primarily young women. Order frequency has been steady at ~4 orders per customer per year (3.8 in 2023, 4.0 in 2024-25, 3.9 LTM March 2026), so growth has come from customer acquisition rather than deeper wallet share. Net revenue per active customer fell from ~US$173 in 2023 to ~US$153 in 2025, reflecting marketplace mix, faster growth in lower-AOV markets and category mix.

**Geographic mix.** Growth has rotated sharply away from the US: US revenue fell 3.5% in 2025 and 9.9% in 1H26, while Europe grew 8.8% in 2025 before falling 13.9% in 2Q26 ahead of the EU duty change, and the rest of the world grew 15.4% in 2025 and 21.6% in 2Q26, led by Latin America. The rest of the world was 44% of revenue in 1H26 versus 39% in 2023.

**Channels and marketing.** SHEIN sells almost exclusively through its own apps and websites (with local-language versions, personalised recommendations and region-specific layouts), supplemented by pop-ups, showrooms and events. Marketing is heavily digital: influencer partnerships (a practice SHEIN pioneered at scale), paid search, product-listing ads, paid social and SEO. Marketing spend rose from 10.7% of revenue in 2024 to 14.8% in 2025 (advertising up from US$4.0bn to US$5.0bn) as the Company fought for engagement against Temu and tariff-driven price increases; it eased to 13.6% in 2Q26 as European advertising was cut ahead of the duty change. 40% of IPO proceeds (~HK$5.3bn) is earmarked for brand-building and performance marketing over 48 months.

**Customer terms.** Payment is taken at order; free shipping above thresholds; generous returns (free return shipping in selected regions); refunds may be issued without return. The prepayment model underpins the negative working-capital cycle (turnover days for net revenues of –12 to –13).

**Seasonality.** The fourth quarter — 11.11, Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas — is the largest promotional window. Working capital builds in the first half and unwinds in the second: operating cash flow was US$2.9bn in 1H25 but –US$39m in 2H25.

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

**Market size.** CIC sizes the global fashion industry (apparel, footwear and accessories, retail sales value ex-VAT) at **US$1,737bn in 2025**, growing at a 2.7% CAGR over 2021-25 and forecast to grow at **3.4% to US$2,049bn by 2030**. Apparel is US$1,370bn and footwear US$367bn. The **online** channel was US$606bn in 2025 (34.9% penetration), forecast to reach **US$792bn by 2030** (38.7% penetration), a 5.5% CAGR — roughly twice offline growth (2.1%).

**Structure.** The industry is highly fragmented: the top 20 players held only ~20.5% of the market in 2025. CIC attributes this to the "trilemma" — the difficulty of simultaneously offering broad assortment, rapid design refresh and efficient inventory — which pushes most players into narrow niches.

**Key trends.**
1. *Secular shift online* driven by mobile payments, global fulfilment, social commerce and recommendation technology.
2. *Individuality and trend velocity:* social media accelerates trend cycles and fragments demand into a long tail, favouring broad, fast-refreshing assortments.
3. *Value beyond fashion:* consumers — and regulators — increasingly scrutinise labour practices, environmental impact and transparency; the EU is legislating on textile waste, product safety and platform accountability.
4. *Supply-side innovation:* small-batch production, real-time inventory management and warehouse automation are becoming table stakes.
5. *Trade-policy re-regulation of cross-border e-commerce:* the US ended de minimis for China (May 2025) and all countries (August 2025); the EU abolished the €150 duty exemption from 1 July 2026 (with a €3-per-item-category flat duty and a forthcoming handling fee); other markets are reviewing similar thresholds. This structurally raises the landed cost of direct-to-consumer cross-border parcels relative to locally stocked inventory.

**Adjacent categories.** CIC sizes home & living at US$1,408bn (online US$282bn → US$374bn by 2030), appliances & electronics at US$1,441bn (online US$659bn → US$1,067bn) and beauty & personal care at US$614bn (online US$171bn → US$264bn). These markets underpin SHEIN's category expansion and marketplace strategy.

**Input costs.** Polyester accounted for 82.7% of SHEIN's raw material by weight in 2025. China's man-made-fibre price index fell from 104.2 (2022) to 94.0 (2025) and China's apparel and footwear PPI was broadly flat (102.0 in 2025 vs 100.0 in 2021), so input costs have been benign; freight and duties are the more important variable costs.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

SHEIN competes primarily with global fashion retailers and, to a lesser extent, with general e-commerce platforms that sell fashion. We group competitors into four categories.

**1. Inditex (Zara) — the benchmark fast-fashion operator.** Market share ~2.5% (CIC). Trailing revenue ~€41bn, EBITDA margin ~24%, net margin ~16%; trades at ~23.5x forward P/E. Inditex's proximity-sourcing model (Spain, Portugal, Morocco, Turkey) delivers two-to-four-week design-to-shelf cycles with a store-plus-online network. SHEIN offers far greater assortment at lower prices; Inditex offers brand equity, quality perception, physical presence and vastly superior margins and returns.

**2. H&M Group.** ~€19bn revenue equivalent (SEK221bn), EBITDA margin ~11%, forward P/E ~19x. H&M's longer lead times and store-heavy model leave it more exposed to markdowns; it has been losing share in value fashion to SHEIN and Primark.

**3. Fast Retailing (Uniqlo).** Share ~1.3%; revenue ¥3.85trn; premium valuation (~39x forward P/E, 22.6x EV/EBITDA) reflecting a basics-led, low-fashion-risk model with strong Asian growth. Limited direct overlap with SHEIN's trend-led assortment but a benchmark for what a global apparel franchise can be worth.

**4. Athletic leaders (Nike, Adidas).** Ranked #1 and #4 by CIC market share; relevant as share benchmarks and as competitors in sportswear, where SHEIN's MUSERA and Glowmode are growing.

**5. PDD Holdings (Temu).** SHEIN's most direct and aggressive competitor since 2023: a fully managed/semi-managed marketplace shipping low-priced Chinese goods worldwide, backed by PDD's ~US$66bn net cash. Temu competes on price across all categories, including fashion, and has contested SHEIN in courts (US antitrust complaint, partially dismissed September 2025; UK copyright and competition cases, with further trials in 2027). Temu faces the same de minimis headwinds. PDD trades at only ~6.8x forward P/E and ~3x EV/EBITDA, anchoring investor expectations for China-linked cross-border platforms.

**6. Alibaba (AliExpress) and other China cross-border platforms.** AliExpress's "Choice" programme competes on price and delivery; Alibaba trades at ~13.6x forward P/E.

**7. Amazon (including Amazon Haul).** Amazon's low-price storefront targets the same value shopper with local fulfilment advantages in the US and Europe; Amazon's scale in logistics is a structural threat as duties erode the cost advantage of direct cross-border shipping.

**8. TikTok Shop.** Social-commerce platforms compete for the same young consumer and the same influencer marketing budgets, and have lowered customer-acquisition barriers for smaller fashion brands.

**9. Online fashion specialists: Zalando, ASOS, Boohoo, Revolve.** Zalando (~€13.5bn revenue; ~12.9x forward P/E) is the leading European online fashion platform and a direct competitor in Europe, now expanding through its About You acquisition. ASOS (loss-making, EV/Sales ~0.5x) and Boohoo have lost share to SHEIN in the UK and US value segments. Revolve (~21x forward P/E) is a US premium online player.

**10. Primark (ABF) and off-price retailers.** Physical value fashion with strong unit economics in Europe; benefits from any increase in cross-border landed costs.

**SHEIN's competitive position.** SHEIN's advantages are assortment breadth (2m+ styles), speed (5-day restocks), inventory efficiency (36-day turnover), cost (value pricing enabled by LATR and Chinese supply-chain density) and a large, engaged customer base (291m). Its disadvantages are thin operating margins (2-4% versus 12-20% for Inditex and Fast Retailing), dependence on cross-border shipping from China (>90% of revenue shipped from Chinese warehouses) at a time when duty regimes are tightening, brand perception issues (quality, sustainability, IP), and regulatory scrutiny that its physical-retail peers largely escape. The competitive threat has shifted from Temu (price) toward platforms with local fulfilment (Amazon) and toward local value retail as duties rise.

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (TAM)

We frame SHEIN's addressable market in three layers:

| Layer | 2025 size | 2030E size | CAGR | SHEIN 2025 revenue / share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global fashion (apparel, footwear, accessories) | US$1,737bn | US$2,049bn | 3.4% | ~1.9% share (CIC, by RSV) |
| Global online fashion | US$606bn | US$792bn | 5.5% | Largest online player |
| Online home & living | US$282bn | US$374bn | 5.8% | Adjacent category |
| Online beauty & personal care | US$171bn | US$264bn | 9.1% | SHEGLAM + marketplace |
| Online appliances & electronics | US$659bn | US$1,067bn | 10.1% | Marketplace only |

*Source: CIC via Prospectus. RSV = retail sales value ex-VAT.*

**Serviceable market.** SHEIN's core serviceable market is online fashion (US$606bn, 2025), where its ~US$27bn of apparel revenue and a portion of its footwear/accessories revenue imply a high-single-digit share. Adding online beauty and home & living takes the serviceable online market to ~US$1.06trn in 2025, rising to ~US$1.43trn by 2030 (6.2% CAGR).

**Share-gain runway.** Even after a decade of hyper-growth SHEIN holds under 2% of global fashion. Management argues its current share is "relatively low". Our base-case model assumes net revenues grow at a 6.1% CAGR over 2026-31E (to US$56bn), modestly ahead of the online fashion market, with orders growing 5-7% a year and revenue per order stabilising as the price-range strategy offsets marketplace mix. Our bull case (8.6% CAGR) assumes Europe re-accelerates after the duty shock and the rest of the world continues to compound in double digits; our bear case (2.8% CAGR) assumes structural share loss in the US and Europe.

**Constraints.** The addressable market is not the binding constraint; trade policy and regulation are. The removal of low-value exemptions in the US and EU — SHEIN's two largest regions and ~60% of 2025 revenue — raises the cost of the direct-from-China model and forces a shift toward local inventory, which carries higher working capital, warehousing and duty costs but faster delivery.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-specific risks

1. **Trade-policy and duty risk (High).** Over 90% of 2025 revenue shipped from Chinese warehouses. The US ended de minimis in 2025 (tariff rates on China-origin goods now 10-87.5% versus 0-62.5% earlier); the EU abolished its €150 exemption from July 2026 and will add a handling fee. SHEIN warns EU trends could be "in line with or exceed" the US experience, where revenue fell 9.9% in 1H26. Duties are booked in fulfilment expenses. Further escalation (e.g., the China-US tariff truce expiring on 10 November 2026) is a direct margin risk.
2. **Regulatory and legal risk (High).** Active matters include: an EU Digital Services Act formal investigation (opened February 2026; DSA fines can reach 6% of global turnover); an FTC investigation of US operations that could lead to a "significant" settlement; a CNIL €150m fine under appeal; €22.5m DGCCRF fines (June 2026) being contested; an Irish DPC inquiry into EU-to-China data transfers (April 2026); and Temu litigation in the US and UK. Provisions were ~US$80m at March 2026 plus the CNIL provision. We deduct a US$1.0bn contingency reserve in our valuation.
3. **Margin-recovery execution risk (High).** Operating margin fell to 2.4% in 1H26 (2Q26: 2.1%) from 5.2% in 1H25 as fulfilment costs rose to 50.4% of revenue. Our base case assumes margins recover to ~3.0% in 2027 and ~4.6% by 2031; failure to pass on duties, sustained high freight, or the costs of local inventory could keep margins near 2%.
4. **Labour, supply-chain and ESG risk (Medium-High).** SHEIN relies on thousands of SME suppliers and their subcontractors; audits cannot detect every violation. Allegations of labour abuses, forced labour in upstream cotton, or chemical-safety failures could trigger import restrictions (e.g., under US forced-labour law), consumer boycotts or ESG exclusions from institutional portfolios.
5. **Brand, IP and product-safety risk (Medium).** SHEIN is regularly subject to IP-infringement claims and product-safety findings (particularly on the marketplace); French authorities pursued SHEIN in 2025 over inappropriate marketplace products. Negative publicity can erode customer trust.
6. **Governance and capital-allocation risk (Medium).** WVR structure gives founders 83% of votes; CEO also chairs the Board; pre-IPO arrangements transferred ~US$3.5bn of cash to preferred holders. Minority shareholders have limited recourse if cash is deployed into low-return acquisitions (SPARC generated losses; Everlane is small).

### Industry and market risks

7. **Competitive intensity (High).** Temu, AliExpress, TikTok Shop and Amazon Haul compete for the same value customer; aggressive subsidies by better-funded platforms could force higher marketing spend (already 14.8% of revenue in 2025).
8. **Fashion and consumer-demand risk (Medium).** SHEIN's customers are young and price-sensitive; a consumer slowdown or inflation shock in key markets would hit order volumes. Management expects the "challenging global consumer environment" to persist near term.
9. **Channel and platform-dependence risk (Medium).** Customer acquisition depends on Google, Meta, TikTok and app stores; changes in algorithms, privacy rules (e.g., cookie-consent enforcement) or ad pricing could raise acquisition costs.

### Financial risks

10. **Freight, oil and FX risk (Medium).** The Iran conflict raised oil prices and freight costs in 1H26 and disrupted Middle East demand (a low-single-digit percentage-point drag on 2026 revenue). SHEIN does not hedge FX; exchange losses were US$151m in 1H26.
11. **Share-overhang and liquidity risk (High, near term).** The IPO float was only 6.6% of shares, much of it placed with existing holders; stabilisation absorbed another 15%. The six-month lock-up for pre-IPO investors (~34% of shares, many with very low cost bases) and cornerstones expires around 1 March 2027 and could weigh on the share price.
12. **Tax risk (Medium).** OECD Pillar Two minimum tax, transfer-pricing scrutiny and potential tax-residency challenges could raise the effective tax rate (core ETR ~26% in 1H26 versus ~21% in 2025).

### Macroeconomic risks

13. **Geopolitical risk (High).** US-China tensions, sanctions or data-security actions (as seen with other China-linked apps) could restrict SHEIN's ability to operate in the US, which is still 22% of revenue.
14. **Global recession / interest-rate risk (Medium).** A recession would depress discretionary spending; conversely, lower interest rates would reduce interest income, which was ~27% of 2027E adjusted pre-tax profit in our model.
