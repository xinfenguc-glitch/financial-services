Cross-cutting themes

1. Strong H1 numbers, de-rated shares

Most of the 17 reported strong H1'26 results, yet the source pack notes every stock trades 28–81% below its 52-week high. China's CSI 300 fell 5.8% in September on worries about tighter policy in major economies and weak domestic demand. Several names (CATL, GigaDevice, Han's Laser, Sungrow) now trade at or near multi-year-low multiples on forward earnings.

2. The AI capex chain has moved down the stack

AI servers are pulling through demand for niche memory (GigaDevice), electronic glass-fibre cloth (China Jushi), PCB drilling equipment (Han's Laser), grid equipment (Sieyuan) and storage (CATL, Sungrow). The question for each is how long pricing power lasts once capacity responds. We are Buy where order books give visibility (Han's) and Hold where earnings already look near peak (GigaDevice, Jushi).

3. Lithium has turned again

China lithium carbonate fell 24% in September to ~RMB122k/t; Wood Mackenzie expects 2026 supply to grow ~28%, and a revised SMM inventory methodology added ~175kt to stock estimates. We model lower H2'26 and FY27 prices for Tianqi and Ganfeng, below broker forecasts in the source pack.

4. Strong CNY is a quiet earnings headwind

USD/CNH is ~6.69. Exporters with dollar receivables (Sany, Sungrow, Sieyuan) booked FX finance losses in H1'26, and dollar-priced commodity producers (Zijin) translate fewer renminbi per unit.

5. A/H spreads matter

Most H-shares trade at large discounts to their A-shares (JL Mag −46%, Ganfeng −45%, Tianqi −44%, Sany −18%), but CATL's H-share trades at a ~41% premium. Where both lines exist we name our preferred line in each report.

6. Robotics and AI models: real growth, stretched prices

Horizon Robotics and Edge Medical combine fast growth with valuations the market has already cut sharply, so we rate them Buy. Leaderdrive (Sell), Z.AI and MiniMax (Hold) price in outcomes several years away.

Company background

Founded in 1993 around the Zijinshan gold-copper deposit in Shanghang, Fujian, Zijin grew under geologist-founder Chen Jinghe from a county gold mine into a global group with mines on five continents. It listed H-shares in Hong Kong in 2003 and A-shares in Shanghai in 2008; its largest shareholder is Minxi Xinghang, the Shanghang county state-asset vehicle. Core assets include Kamoa-Kakula (DRC, with Ivanhoe), the Serbian copper complex (Bor and Čukaru Peki), Julong copper in Tibet, and gold mines in Colombia, Suriname, Australia and Kazakhstan. In 2025 its overseas gold assets were spun out as Zijin Gold International in Hong Kong, in which Zijin keeps control.

Business model

Zijin earns the spread between metal prices and its unit costs on self-mined output, which generates most of its gross profit. Refining and trading add revenue at thin margins (the 19% 'other' slice in the mix chart). Its edge is in-house geology and mining engineering. That lets it buy under-developed or distressed deposits cheaply, often in higher-risk jurisdictions, then build them faster and at lower capital cost than peers. Gold (41% of H1'26 sales) and copper (33%) dominate; zinc/lead, silver and a fast-growing lithium arm (H1 output up ~5x) make up the rest. Operating cash flow funds greenfield expansion, bolt-on M&A and a 35% dividend payout.

Competitive landscape

In copper, Zijin's 1.09Mt of 2025 mined output places it among the world's top five producers, alongside BHP, Freeport and Codelco; in gold, ~90t (~2.9Moz) puts it in the global top ten behind Newmont, Barrick and Agnico Eagle. Management targets a top-three ranking in both by 2028. Compared with Western majors, it grows faster, spends less capital per tonne and has Chinese bank funding. The trade-offs are a riskier footprint (DRC, Serbia, Colombia, Suriname) and a lower payout. Domestic comparables are CMOC (copper/cobalt) and Shandong Gold/Zhaojin (gold).

Addressable market (TAM)

Zijin's addressable market is global mined copper and gold supply. Refined copper consumption runs at ~27Mt a year (~US$400bn at current prices). Electrification, grid build-out and AI data-centre power demand are colliding with declining ore grades and slow mine development, which supports a structural deficit narrative. Annual gold mine supply of ~3,600t is worth ~US$485bn at today's price, with central-bank and ETF demand underpinning prices. Zijin's 2026 targets equal roughly 4–5% of world copper and ~3% of world gold mine supply.

Company background

Sany was founded in 1989 by Liang Wengen and three partners in Lianyuan, Hunan, and is now headquartered in Changsha. It began in welding materials before moving into concrete pumps and excavators. It listed in Shanghai in 2003 and added a Hong Kong H-share listing in 2025; Liang remains the controlling shareholder through Sany Group. Sany has been China's leading excavator brand since 2011 and sells in more than 180 countries. Its overseas manufacturing includes Putzmeister in Germany (acquired 2012) and plants in India, Indonesia, the US and Brazil.

Business model

Sany makes and sells excavators (~40% of H1'26 revenue), concrete machinery, cranes, piling and road machinery through dealers and direct sales. It supports sales with customer financing (instalments and leases) and a growing parts-and-service business. Profitability depends on volume (fixed-cost absorption), mix and pricing discipline: large excavators, mining equipment and overseas units carry higher margins. Electrified products and digital fleet management are differentiators. Overseas growth relies on local plants and a dealer network built over the past decade.

Competitive landscape

At home Sany competes with XCMG, Zoomlion, LiuGong, SDLG and Sunward, and foreign brands' share of China's excavator market has shrunk sharply over the past decade. Globally the benchmarks are Caterpillar, Komatsu, Deere, Volvo CE, Hitachi CM and Liebherr. Sany wins on price (Chinese machines typically sell at a meaningful discount to Japanese and US brands), product breadth and speed of dealer expansion. Its gaps are brand perception in North America and Western Europe, after-market density, and exposure to trade barriers. China's excavator exports now roughly equal domestic sales (116,739 vs 118,518 units in 2025).

Addressable market (TAM)

Global construction equipment is roughly a US$200–250bn market in annual OEM sales. China sold 235,257 excavators in 2025 (+17%), half of them exported, and 191,557 in Jan–Aug 2026 (+24%). Domestic demand rests on fleet replacement and infrastructure and water-conservancy spending; exports ride emerging-market mining and infrastructure. Sany's ~US$12.5bn of FY25 revenue is a mid-single-digit share of the global pool, which leaves room to grow.

Company background

Founded in Shanghai in 1993 by Dong Zengping, Sieyuan started in power-system test equipment and capacitors. It now offers a full T&D portfolio: high-voltage switchgear (GIS), transformers, reactors, protection and automation, reactive-power compensation (SVG/STATCOM), energy storage and EPC. It listed in Shenzhen in 2004. As a privately controlled company it competes against State Grid-affiliated SOEs. In December 2025 it announced a Hong Kong H-share listing of up to 15% of its share capital, which still awaits completion.

Business model

Sieyuan sells mainly through competitive tenders run by State Grid, China Southern Grid, power generators and industrial users, and increasingly to overseas utilities and EPC contractors (29.5% of H1'26 revenue). Revenue follows order intake with a 6–18-month lag; the 2026 target is RMB37.5bn of new orders (+30%) and RMB27bn of revenue (+25%). Switchgear (40% of H1'26 revenue) and transformers (21%) earn gross margins around or above 30%. Storage systems (13%) are a low-margin integration business. Working capital is meaningful, but the company has stayed net cash.

Competitive landscape

At home Sieyuan competes with State Grid-affiliated Pinggao and XJ Electric, China XD Electric, TBEA (transformers) and NARI (secondary equipment). Abroad it faces Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, GE Vernova, Schneider, HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung and LS Electric. Its edge is breadth, delivery speed and cost. China's transformer exports rose 35% to US$9.0bn in 2025 because Western capacity could not keep up. The main pressure points are price in domestic tenders and the commoditised storage segment.

Addressable market (TAM)

China's grid spending is at record highs: State Grid alone plans more than RMB650bn a year, and the 15th Five-Year Plan points to over RMB5tn for 2026–30. Globally, renewables integration, electrification and AI data-centre load are driving a multi-year upcycle in transformers and switchgear, with US and European lead times of two years or more. Sieyuan's FY25 revenue of RMB21.5bn is a small slice of this pool, so share gains abroad are the swing factor.
