# Valuing defence companies

## Pick the measure for the cycle stage

| Stage | What investors can see | Measures that work |
|---|---|---|
| Orders surging, little earnings impact yet | Contract wins, framework agreements, pipeline | EV / backlog, market cap / backlog, pipeline scenarios |
| Orders converting to revenue | Revenue growth, margin trend, cash from advances | Forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT on two-to-three-year estimates |
| Mature, steady deliveries | Stable margins and cash | FCF yield, dividend capacity, DCF |

State which stage the company is in and why, and use more than one measure.

## Backlog-based comparison

- Put EV / backlog next to **backlog cover** (years) and the **expected margin** on that backlog. Backlog at a mid-single-digit margin is worth far less than the same backlog at mid-teens.
- Adjust for backlog quality: funded vs unfunded, fixed-price vs cost-type, domestic vs export, and how much is options or frameworks.
- Compare peers in the same domain and chain position. A land-systems prime and a missile-component supplier shouldn't share a multiple just because both are "defence".

## Probability-weighted export pipeline

Use this when the thesis depends on orders not yet signed. Build one row per opportunity:

| Customer | Programme | Units | Unit price | Contract value | Expected award | Win probability | Delivery years | Revenue by year (all wins) | Revenue by year (weighted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:
- **Tie probabilities to evidence.** For example: signed = 100%; selected and in negotiation = high; shortlisted = medium; open competition = low; speculative = near zero. Write down the evidence behind each probability.
- **Unit prices.** Take them from comparable signed contracts, not list prices. Note whether they include training, spares, munitions and support.
- **Delivery.** Spread deliveries over realistic lead times and check the total against the company's stated production capacity. If the schedule needs more units a year than the factories can make, it's wrong.
- **Show both lines.** The all-wins bull case and the probability-weighted base case. Report the implied valuation (e.g. P/E on an out-year) under each.

## Market sizing (TAM)

**Top-down.** Defence budget × procurement share × share spent on the relevant category × share realistically open to this supplier. That last share depends on import dependence, political alignment and local-content rules.

**Bottom-up.** Installed inventory × share that is ageing or obsolete × replacement rate over the period × unit price of the replacement system. Inventory counts come from sources such as the IISS Military Balance.

Filter target countries on evidence rather than including every possible buyer:
- restocking urgency
- existing relationship with the supplier country
- announced procurement plans or budgets

Run both methods and explain any gap between them.

## Sum of the parts

For groups with defence inside a broader industrial or holding structure (heavy-industry conglomerates, family groups with listed subsidiaries):

- Value the defence arm on defence peer multiples, and the rest on their own peers.
- Apply a holding discount where minority stakes or cross-holdings exist, and state the size.
- Note where segment disclosure is too thin to separate defence profit. Estimate it, and label the estimate.

## Premium and discount checklist

Justify any premium or discount against the peer group:

- earnings growth and order momentum
- margin level and direction (export mix, contract mix)
- single-product or single-customer risk
- backlog quality and cover
- balance sheet and cash conversion (including how much depends on advances)
- governance, free float and listing structure
- geopolitical exposure of the export book

## Signposts

List the events that would change the view, with dates where known:

- budget votes
- contract awards and down-selects
- export licence decisions
- capacity milestones
- ceasefire or escalation
- re-competitions

Update the pipeline table when they happen.
