# Defence KPIs: definitions and pitfalls

## Order book

| Measure | Definition | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|
| **Backlog (total)** | Value of signed contracts not yet recognised as revenue. | Definitions vary by company and country. Some include unexercised options or framework ceilings; most don't. Read the footnote. |
| **Funded backlog** | Portion with appropriated money obligated (a US distinction). | A large unfunded share is at risk if budgets slip. |
| **Remaining performance obligations (RPO)** | The IFRS 15 / ASC 606 measure. | Often differs from the company's own "backlog"; use one consistently across peers. |
| **Book-to-bill** | Orders divided by revenue for the period. | Very lumpy: one large export contract can swing a quarter. Judge on trailing four quarters or a full year. |
| **Backlog cover** | Backlog divided by trailing-twelve-month revenue, in years. | High cover means visibility, not profitability. Check the margin and delivery schedule of what's in it. |
| **Framework / IDIQ ceilings** | Maximum value a customer may order under an umbrella contract. | Not backlog. Only task orders placed against it count. |

## Contract types and margin risk

| Type | Who carries cost risk | Typical margin profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Firm fixed-price (FFP)** | Contractor | Highest upside and highest overrun risk. Common in production and exports. |
| **Fixed-price incentive (FPIF)** | Shared up to a ceiling | Contractor shares savings and overruns. |
| **Cost-plus fixed fee (CPFF)** | Customer | Low, stable fee. Common in development. |
| **Cost-plus incentive / award fee (CPIF/CPAF)** | Customer | Fee varies with cost or performance. |
| **Time & materials** | Mostly customer | Services-like. |

- Development work is usually cost-type and production usually fixed-price. A company moving a programme from development into fixed-price production takes on new risk.
- **Estimate at completion (EAC).** Percentage-of-completion accounting books profit as work progresses using an estimated final cost. When that estimate changes, the cumulative catch-up hits the current period. Track net favourable minus unfavourable EAC adjustments as a share of revenue. A run of unfavourable adjustments on one programme, or a forward loss provision, is an early warning.
- **US pension accounting.** US primes recover pension cost through contract pricing under government Cost Accounting Standards (CAS), which differs from the financial-reporting charge. The FAS/CAS adjustment can move reported operating profit without changing the business; compare segment margins before it.

## Customer, programme and export mix

- **Home-government share.** Stable, but domestic contracts are often cost-plus or price-controlled, so margins are lower.
- **Largest-programme share.** Above roughly a fifth of revenue, a single cancellation or re-competition becomes a thesis risk.
- **Export share.** Exports can carry higher margins and more pricing power when buyers need fast delivery. They also bring licence risk, offsets and local-production demands. Look at the destination mix, not just the total.
- **Margin by customer type.** If the company discloses it, model margins by destination. A mix shift toward exports can lift group margins faster than volume.

## Cash and capital

- **Customer advances and progress payments.** Advances create negative working capital. Cash conversion jumps when order intake surges, then falls as advances are used up in production. Don't extrapolate a peak year.
- **Contract assets and unbilled receivables.** Rising balances mean revenue is recognised ahead of billing. Check against milestones.
- **Capex for capacity.** Rearmament cycles need new lines, energetics facilities and test infrastructure. Capex as a share of sales can rise well above history for several years, and some of it is government co-funded. Note who pays.
- **R&D.** Separate self-funded (company-funded, US "IRAD") from customer-funded development. Self-funded R&D rising as a share of sales depresses margins today but builds export products.

## Position in the value chain

| Position | Economics | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Prime / platform integrator** | Large programmes, lower margins, integration and fixed-price risk | Programme concentration, overruns |
| **Tier-1 subsystem supplier** | Higher margins, content on many platforms | Platform volume, prime pricing pressure |
| **Merchant component supplier** | Diversified across programmes and primes | Second-sourcing, customer concentration among a few primes |
| **Bottleneck-input holder** (energetics, rocket motors, seekers) | Pricing power, durable moat | Capacity-expansion execution, permits |
| **Services / MRO** | Recurring, tied to fleet size and age | Contract re-competition |
