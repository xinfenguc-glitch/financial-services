# Workflow: China Credit-Risk Ladder

**Question answered**: How far toward the core of the system has default risk travelled, and which guarantees are still credible?
**Frameworks**: wright-deleveraging ch01 (periphery-to-core contagion), ch04 (defaults, geographic counterparty risk), ch06 (partial moral-hazard break, guarantee erosion as the crisis path); patterns.md "Contagion-Ladder Mapping" and "Guarantee-Credibility Test".

## Step 1: Populate the status column
For each rung, record recent defaults, extensions or restructurings; spreads and issuance access; and the official response (rescue, merger, managed restructuring or none). Use current data or news and cite dates.

## Step 2: Guarantee-credibility scoring
For the top 3–5 exposures in question, score 1–5 on each dimension:
| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Central, provincial, prefecture, county or private? |
| Guarantor capacity | Local land-sale trend, fund-budget balance, SRB refinancing share, LGFV land-purchase share |
| Peer precedent | Has a comparable entity in the same province or sector defaulted recently? |
| Leader alignment | Does top-level rhetoric support or oppose the sector? |
| Rescue history | Did Beijing rescue the last comparable failure? |

## Step 3: Contagion checks
- **Geographic**: after a local default, are the province's other issuers shut out (the Henan precedent after Yongcheng)?
- **Funding**: interbank stress such as NCD rates, DR007 spikes and tiering between big and small banks (the June 2013 and Sealand pattern).
- **Fire-sale channel**: are safe assets being sold for liquidity (government and policy-bank bonds)?
- **Household channel**: are presale completions, mortgage prepayments or delinquencies moving?

## Step 4: Verdict
- **Contained**: defaults stay on rungs ≤5, funding markets are calm and LGFV access is intact.
- **Advancing**: new defaults reach rung 6 or 7, or several provinces lose market access.
- **Systemic watch**: an LGFV public-bond default plus interbank tiering, or a rescue that markets judge to be insufficient.

Remember Wright's warning: Beijing has crisis tools but prefers to use them quietly, so look for indirect support (bank "guidance", PBOC relending, debt swaps) rather than announced bailouts.
