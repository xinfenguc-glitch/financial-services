# Workflow: Decode a Chinese Policy Signal

**Question answered**: What does this statement or meeting mean for policy direction, how durable is it, and who is behind it?
**Frameworks**: wright-deleveraging ch03 (campaign governance, the coalition word, policy-signal tells), ch06 (centralization and guarantees), ch07 (credibility under centralization); patterns.md "Policy-Signal Decoding".

## Step 1: Classify the channel and its weight
| Channel | Typical weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Top-leader speech or slogan the leader personally owns | Highest under centralization | e.g. "houses are for living, not for speculation". Hard to reverse unless the leader reverses it. |
| *People's Daily* "authoritative personage" or front-page commentary | Very high; often marks a pivot | May 2016 launched deleveraging |
| Politburo economic readout (roughly quarterly) | High; sets the quarter's priorities | Track phrasing changes quarter to quarter |
| Central Economic Work Conference / Central Financial Work Conference | High; multi-year | The 2017 NFWC framed deleveraging as long-term |
| Government work report (March NPC) | High for targets | The **GDP target is the key tell** (ch07) |
| PBOC Monetary Policy Report and its special columns | Medium-high; technocratic intent | Special columns are PBOC staff's own choice of topic |
| Xinhua commentary series | Medium; amplification | Seven commentaries in May 2017 tied financial and national security |
| Ministry or regulator circular | Medium; implementation | Watch for drafts → final versions and loosened grace periods |

## Step 2: Text analysis
1. Diff against the previous comparable release. Note added, removed and reordered phrases.
2. Count loaded words (e.g. "risk", "bubble", "leverage", "stabilize growth", "expand domestic demand", "debt resolution") against the previous release.
3. Flag new slogans and numbered lists ("three major battles").
4. Note what is **missing**. Dropped phrases often matter more than added ones.

## Step 3: Political economy
- **Who benefits from each reading?** (technocrats, SOEs, localities, planners; the "coalition word" test)
- **Consensus or campaign?** Does it read like broad bureaucratic mobilization, or a top-down crackdown that will be sharp and hard to reverse?
- **Guarantee implications**: does it strengthen or weaken implied state support for a sector (property, LGFVs, platforms, banks)?
- **Persistence**: has the signal recurred across at least two channels or quarters? A single statement is weak.

## Step 4: Map to policy path
Map the signal to Wright's four options (double down / save localities / save property / abandon). Then list the **policy instruments** likely to follow, such as RRR, policy rates, quotas, bond quotas, regulatory rules or fiscal transfers, with expected timing.

## Output template
```
SIGNAL READ — [source, date]
Channel weight: [H/M/L] | Signal type: [pivot / reinforcement / walk-back]
What changed vs last release: [bullets with quoted phrases]
Who's driving it: [leader / technocrats / coalition]
Guarantee implications: [sector → stronger/weaker]
Maps to Wright option: [ ] | Durability: [ ]
Expected follow-through (instrument, timing): [ ]
Market read-through (inference): rates [ ], CNY [ ], credit spreads [ ], property [ ], commodities [ ]
```
