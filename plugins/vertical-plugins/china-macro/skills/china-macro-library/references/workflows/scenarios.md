# Workflow: Beijing Policy Scenarios

**Question answered**: Which strategic path is Beijing on, and what does it imply for rates, CNY, credit, property, commodities and trade?
**Frameworks**: wright-deleveraging ch07 (four options, best path, six outcomes, US implications), ch06 (necessary-but-insufficient verdict); cheatsheet.md trade-off matrix.

## Step 1: Evidence inventory
Collect the past 6–12 months of policy evidence:
- **Targets**: the GDP growth target and the fiscal deficit and bond quotas from the latest government work report.
- **Credit policy**: quota language, PBOC rate and RRR moves, window guidance, any capital-ratio relief.
- **Local debt**: debt swaps, recognition of hidden debt, LGFV restructuring, central transfers.
- **Property**: developer financing relief ("whitelists"), down-payment and mortgage-rate changes, purchase-restriction removals, inventory buybacks, completion funds.
- **Households**: transfers, safety-net and pension changes, consumption subsidies.
- **Markets and FX**: capital-market opening, inflow programs, FX intervention behavior.
Use the timeline's post-report section and fresh research, citing dates.

## Step 2: Score each option
| Option (Wright ch07) | Evidence for | Evidence against | Weight 0–100% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Double down on deleveraging | GDP target cut or dropped; new limits on LGFVs, SOEs and shadow banks; property curbs kept | | |
| 2. Save local governments | Explicit recognition or swap of hidden debt; central bonds replacing LGFV debt; PBOC balance-sheet support | | |
| 3. Save the property sector | Developer liquidity; demand incentives; target held near 5% | | |
| 4. Abandon deleveraging | Quotas dropped; growth target raised to 6–7%; broad bailouts; capital relief | | |
Weights should sum to 100% because options can blend. Also score progress on Wright's **best path** (credit only slightly above nominal GDP; local-debt fiscalization; household-income support; market opening).

## Step 3: Market implications
State these as your inference, anchored to Wright's six outcomes.
| Variable | Wright long-run direction | Scenario adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Credit growth | Single digits, slowing | Option 4: re-accelerates, then stress within 1–2 years |
| Policy and government bond rates | Lower over time; PBOC balance-sheet use | Option 2: more monetization |
| CNY | Gradual depreciation (low rates, outflows, 98% RMB deposits, reserves ~8% of M2) | Opening plus inflows (best path) slows it |
| Commodities (iron ore, copper, aluminum, refined products) | Weaker import demand; property ~40–50% of previous-decade activity | Option 3 or 4: temporary bounce |
| Trade balance | Persistent surpluses; DM deficits | A weaker CNY widens them |
| Credit spreads | Rising differentiation by guarantor quality | Option 2 compresses LGFV spreads |
| Property | Structural decline, cyclical bounces | Option 3: limited traction (high ownership, few new entrants) |

## Step 4: Watch-list and triggers
List 5–8 observable triggers that would shift the weights, such as the next GDP target, an LGFV public-bond default, an announced central-bond swap size, the mortgage-rate floor, household transfer programs and FX-reserve drawdowns.
