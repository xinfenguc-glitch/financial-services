# Patterns and Techniques: *Grasping Shadows* (Wright, 2023)

## Risk-Relocation Trace
**When to use**: any Chinese tightening or crackdown on a financing channel.
**How**: (1) name the squeezed channel; (2) list who borrowed through it, including rate-insensitive or guaranteed borrowers; (3) find their substitute funding (another nonbank, households, ABS, perpetual bonds); (4) ask whether the substitute is less regulated or less visible; (5) set a watch on the substitute.
**Trade-offs**: needs granular flow data. Substitutes can take one or two years to show up in data (presales 2017–18 led to the 2021 crash).

## Contagion-Ladder Mapping
**When to use**: assessing systemic risk or default news.
**How**: place each default on the ladder: P2P → small banks → local SOEs → trusts → private developers → households and mortgages → LGFV public bonds → large banks. Ask whether the new default is a rung closer to the core and whether Beijing intervened, which tells you whether the guarantee is still alive.
**Trade-offs**: rung order is empirical (2018–22), not a law. Centralization can make jumps abrupt.

## Guarantee-Credibility Test
**When to use**: pricing SOE, LGFV or bank credit.
**How**: score (a) explicit or implicit state ownership; (b) local guarantor fiscal strength (land sales, fund-budget balance); (c) recent defaults in the same province or sector; (d) leader rhetoric aligned with or against the sector; (e) whether Beijing rescued the last comparable failure.
**Trade-offs**: under centralization, (d) can override everything else overnight.

## Credit Measurement Triangulation
**When to use**: judging China's credit cycle.
**How**: compare (1) bank-asset growth, (2) TSF growth (note methodology breaks in July 2018 and December 2019), (3) the change in claims on banks + NBFIs, and (4) "shares and other investments" as a share of uses of funds. Compute estimated interest on credit against nominal GDP growth.
**Trade-offs**: bank assets also include claims on government and FX. Revised TSF is not comparable over time without adjustment.

## Bank Balance-Sheet Shadow Scan
**When to use**: single-bank or sector analysis.
**How**: ratios: (claims on banks + NBFIs + receivables)/assets; loans/assets; deposits/liabilities; interbank liabilities/liabilities; NCD share; WMP balance. Healthy direction: loans/assets ↑, deposits/liabilities ↑, receivables ↓.
**Trade-offs**: IFRS 9 changed receivables reporting. Off-balance WMPs need separate disclosure.

## Policy-Signal Decoding
**When to use**: reading Politburo readouts, PBOC Monetary Policy Reports (MPRs), Xinhua series, Financial Work Conference communiqués or anonymous commentary.
**How**: (1) identify the channel (front-page "authoritative personage" > Politburo > PBOC MPR special column > ministry); (2) count loaded words against the previous release ("bubble" 16 vs 7; "risk" 28 times); (3) find new slogans ("three major battles", "aggregates"); (4) check persistence across several quarters; (5) under centralization, check whether the leader personally owns the phrase.
**Trade-offs**: word counts are noisy. Persistence matters more than any single statement.

## Monetary-Tightening Read-Through
**When to use**: PBOC operations look tighter but the policy rate is unchanged.
**How**: watch the overnight/7-day repo level and volatility, OMO tenor mix (7-day vs 14-day), MLF volumes, and the NCD rate and issuance. Expect safe-asset selling (government and policy-bank bonds) first.
**Trade-offs**: tightening can trigger accidents (Sealand). The PBOC may reverse quickly.

## Presale-Substitution Monitor
**When to use**: property-sector or developer credit analysis.
**How**: track new starts against completions, presale vs completed-home sales (area and revenue), presale revenue as a share of developer funding, and mortgage share of bank loans. Widening gaps mean developers are prefunding with buyers' money.
**Trade-offs**: data is national. Escrow enforcement varies by locality.

## Alternative-Activity Check
**When to use**: official GDP looks too smooth.
**How**: cross-check against industrial value-added, unit output series (autos, white goods, completions), weighted-output trackers like R-CAT, and credit-impulse models.
**Trade-offs**: weighting choices matter, and consumer services are under-covered.

## Scorecard-on-Own-Terms
**When to use**: evaluating any Chinese policy campaign.
**How**: list Beijing's stated objectives, pick one metric per objective, and grade success, partial or failure. Then add the unintended consequences separately.
**Trade-offs**: stated objectives may be strategically vague (the "coalition word" problem).

## Strategic-Option Mapping
**When to use**: forecasting the policy path or building scenarios.
**How**: classify current measures under the four options (double down, save localities, save property, abandon). Use the GDP target as the tell, then derive market implications for rates, CNY, commodities, credit spreads and the trade balance.
**Trade-offs**: options can be blended, and the abandon option will never be announced.
