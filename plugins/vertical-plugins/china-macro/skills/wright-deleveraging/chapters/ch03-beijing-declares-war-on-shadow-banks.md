# Chapter 3: Beijing Declares War on Shadow Banks

## Core Idea
The campaign moved through four stages: political messaging, PBOC monetary tightening, comprehensive regulation targeting *funding* (WMPs and NCDs), and bureaucratic reorganization plus local-debt discipline. Monetary tightening was necessary but insufficient and risky. Regulation did the real work. The most effective tool of all was allowing defaults, so that credit risk got priced.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Campaign-style governance**: mobilizing bureaucracies through propaganda and persistent high-level messaging. This is not mass mobilization as in the 1950s and 1960s.
  - Signals: a front-page "authoritative personage" interview, then Politburo readouts, Xinhua commentary series, PBOC report language and the five-yearly financial work conference.
  - Cost: bandwidth. Officials can focus intensely on only a few targets at once.
- **"Deleveraging" as a coalition word**: the campaign launched only because each camp read the term its own way:
  - technocrats (PBOC, CBRC): cut credit-to-GDP;
  - SOE advocates: cut SOE balance-sheet debt;
  - planners: cut the debt-service drag on growth.
  - The Politburo's endorsement let all three push at once.
- **Quiet tightening vs loud regulation**: the PBOC preferred small incremental moves that needed few internal approvals. Louder CBRC and CIRC activity risked a backlash from SOEs and localities.
- **Monetary → regulatory sequencing**: tighten liquidity to break leveraged carry, then regulate funding instruments. Once the regulatory structure holds, *ease* monetary policy (from 2018) to contain stress.
- **"Equal and opposite reaction"**: every constraint triggered a work-around. NCDs replaced repo, structured deposits replaced WMPs, asset-backed securities (ABS) served developers, and perpetual bonds served SOEs.

## Key Concepts
- **People's Daily, 9 May 2016**: "A tree cannot grow to the sky, and high leverage carries high risks." Leverage was called the "original sin", and the article warned that "big stimulus will only result in bubbles". The speaker was presumed to be Liu He.
- **Policy-signal tells**: the PBOC's Q4 2016 monetary policy report used "bubble" 16 times, against 7 in the previous report. After the July 2017 conference, a 3,300-word Xinhua piece used "risk" 28 times and "regulation" 22 times. The December 2017 Politburo meeting named **three major battles**: financial risk, poverty and pollution.
- **Macroprudential Assessment (MPA)**: quarterly A/B/C bank grades. Off-balance WMPs were included from December 2016.
- **Asset management rules**: drafts in February 2017 and November 2017, final version in April 2018. They banned guarantees, required mark-to-market, allowed one layer of intermediation and barred investment in restricted sectors. The cash-type WMP exception became a loophole that was closed in 2021.
- **"334 checks" (2017)**: three CBRC circulars covering compliance, arbitrage through NCDs and WMPs, and "innovation" (shadow banking). Later made a standing mechanism.
- **Liquidity Matching Ratio (LMR, 2018)**: wholesale funding under 3 months, including NCDs, gets *zero* weight. A pure deposit-and-loan bank passes easily.
- **NCDs as interbank liabilities**: announced in August 2017 and effective from 2018, which brought them under the one-third cap on interbank liabilities.
- **WMP subsidiaries (December 2018)**: WMPs ring-fenced from banks' own books.
- **FSDC (November 2017)**: the Financial Stability and Development Committee under the State Council, led by Liu He with the PBOC running daily operations. **CBIRC** (2018) merged the CBRC and CIRC.
- **Local implicit debt**: the RMB 12.2 trillion swap-bond program (2015–18) under-captured hidden debt. From 2018 localities had to disclose implicit debt and repay it within 3–5 years.
- **SOE target (September 2018)**: cut leverage by 2 percentage points from 2017 levels by end-2020. Debt-for-equity swaps stalled, and SOEs issued perpetual bonds instead.

## Mental Models
- Treat **policy-rate changes as lagging**. The PBOC tightened through *operations* (14-day reverse repos, guidance to borrow longer, more MLF) months before its first formal hike.
- Expect **tightening to sell the safest assets first**. Nonbanks starved of liquidity dump government and policy-bank bonds because those are the most liquid.
- **Target funding instruments, not lenders**, to escape the whack-a-mole problem.
- **Defaults are the real deleveraging tool**, and the most dangerous one. A broken guarantee in one asset class invites doubt about all of them.

## Anti-patterns
- **Monetary tightening in a highly indebted, guarantee-dependent system**: it bred new funding risks (NCDs) and market accidents without slowing aggregate credit.
- **Declaring targets you cannot enforce** (SOE leverage): firms meet them cosmetically, for example with perpetual bonds classified as equity.

## Worked Example: the PBOC's tightening sequence and its accidents
| Date | Move | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 24 Aug 2016 | 14-day reverse repos at 2.4% (vs 7-day at 2.25%) | Uncertainty; overnight rate up to 2.1–2.2% |
| Late 2016 | Guidance to cut overnight borrowing; more MLF | Overnight 2.0–2.1% (Jul–Aug) → 2.4–2.7% (Dec) |
| Dec 2016 | **Sealand Securities** threatens to walk away from entrusted bond deals | Banks stop lending to nonbanks; PBOC extends trading hours; CSRC holds weekend creditor meeting |
| Feb 2017–Apr 2018 | Four OMO rate hikes, 30 bp in total | Bond yields up, issuance down |
| Oct–Nov 2017 | Bond selloff | Policy banks can't issue below 5% against ~4% loan yields; policy-bank issuance −30% in 2017 |
| End-2017 | Assessment | Shadow channels shrinking, aggregate credit barely slowing, property still booming → shift to regulation |

## Key Takeaways
1. Watch the *funding* side (WMPs, NCDs, interbank). That is where the 2017–18 rules bit.
2. Monetary tightening should be judged by money-market volatility and term structure, not just the policy rate.
3. A political mandate (Politburo, FSDC) was the precondition for regulatory success.
4. Regulation did not stop after 2018: the three red lines (August 2020), trust-loan cuts targeting about RMB 1 trillion (2021) and cash-type WMP rules (late 2021) followed.
5. As long as implicit guarantees exist, leverage bounces back once policy eases.
