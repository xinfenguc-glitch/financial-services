# Chapter 4: Deleveraging and China's Financial System

## Core Idea
Using data from 42 banks (about 66% of system assets) plus PBOC, CBIRC and nonbank series, Wright shows that the campaign succeeded on its primary objective. Shadow credit contracted outright, funding moved back to deposits and loans moved back onto bank balance sheets. The costs were sharply slower aggregate credit, regional credit divergence and the start of real defaults.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The five financial-system effects** (use as a checklist):
  1. Overall credit and debt growth slowed, and shadow credit contracted outright.
  2. Non-deposit liabilities (WMPs, interbank funding) contracted and were replaced by deposits.
  3. Loans migrated back onto bank balance sheets, replacing investment receivables.
  4. Credit slowed most in the northeast and western provinces. The coastal southeast kept access.
  5. Defaults spread: P2P (2017–18), small banks (2019), corporate bonds (2018–20), developers and trusts (2021).
- **Measure credit with bank assets, not just TSF**: total social financing (TSF) captures only part of shadow credit (trust loans, entrusted loans, undiscounted acceptances). Bank assets, from the "other depository corporations" balance sheet, include claims on banks and nonbanks, where relabeled shadow assets sat. In 2017–18 bank assets showed the sharper (truer) slowdown.
- **Geographic counterparty risk**: once local SOEs default, entire provinces can be cut off. Credit pricing now depends on the perceived strength of the local guarantor.
- **Breaking moral hazard, two ways**: (a) political signal: by declaring war on shadow channels, Beijing made them visibly unprotected; (b) mechanical: slower credit means fewer refinancing options, which produces defaults whatever the political signal.

## Key Concepts
- **Bank asset growth**: averaged 17.5% (2009–16), then 8.4% (2017) and 6.8% (2018), a record low. Aggregate leverage fell for one of the first times.
- **Claims on banks plus nonbanks**: +RMB 9.9 trillion (2015) and +9.0 trillion (2016), then −0.45 trillion (2017) and −3.1 trillion (2018). New bank assets fell from RMB 31.2 trillion (2016) to 19.3 trillion (2017) and 17.0 trillion (2018).
- **Corporate credit (loans + bonds + shadow claims)**: grew about 4% by early 2018, against an 18.3% average in 2012–16.
- **Deposits / liabilities**: 79.6% (2012), 71.4% (2017), 74.6% (2021). At about RMB 250 trillion of liabilities, each percentage point is roughly RMB 2.5 trillion.
- **Interbank liabilities**: peaked at 21.5% of liabilities in 2016. Interbank deposits fell from RMB 19.2 trillion (2016) to 16.1 trillion (2018).
- **WMP balance**: RMB 23.1 trillion (2016) to 22.0 trillion (2018), i.e. rolled over but no longer growing. It rebounded in 2020–21 as cash-type products.
- **Structured deposits**: a WMP substitute and arbitrage vehicle. Once regulated, savings moved into time deposits.
- **Loans / assets**: 46.3% (2016) to 55.4% (2021).
- **Investment receivables** (trust beneficiary rights, directional asset-management plans): 4.8% of assets (2012), 18.3% (2016), 12.2% two years later. Reporting changed under IFRS 9.
- **"Shares and other investments"** in the PBOC sources-and-uses data: rose RMB 20.8 trillion over 2011–16, peaking at 12.9% of uses in **February 2017**, and roughly halved by 2021.
- **Nonbank (NBFI) assets**: rose an estimated RMB 51 trillion in 2013–16 and fell in 2017–18, led by trusts.
- **WMP asset mix**: nonstandard credit fell from 27.5% (2013) to 8.4% (2021). Bonds rose to 68.4%.
- **Regional TSF growth since end-2018**: about 4% in the northeast and west, about 13% in the coastal southeast.

## Mental Models
- Think of the campaign as **converting off-balance-sheet claims into visible loans**. Transparency and funding stability go up, while capital needs and measured loan growth also go up.
- Use **"claims on banks + NBFIs" swinging negative** as the cleanest single indicator of shadow-credit contraction.
- When credit tightens, **lenders discriminate by geography and asset class**. Watch interior provinces' bond issuance after any local SOE default.

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying on TSF alone**: it understates shadow-credit swings and was later revised (see ch06).
- **Assuming WMP stagnation means risk is gone**: products shifted form (structured deposits, cash-type WMPs) until each was regulated in turn.

## Worked Example: reading a bank balance sheet for shadow exposure
1. Pull assets: loans, claims on other banks, claims on nonbank financial institutions, and investment receivables or "shares and other investments".
2. Compute (claims on banks + NBFIs + receivables) as a share of total assets. A rising share means a growing channel business. 2012–16 is the template: 4.8% to 18.3%.
3. Pull liabilities: deposits, interbank deposits, interbank borrowing, NCDs ("interbank bonds"). A falling deposit share plus a rising interbank share means fragile funding (the June 2013 setup).
4. Check the direction after a regulatory push. Healthy deleveraging shows loans/assets ↑, deposits/liabilities ↑, receivables ↓ and claims on NBFIs ↓. The surveyed banks showed exactly this pattern in 2017–21.

## Key Takeaways
1. The campaign really did shrink shadow banking. Every measure contracted in 2017–18.
2. Banks became more transparent and deposit-funded, so a June-2013 run became far less likely.
3. Credit slowed *more* than Beijing intended, partly because it never knew how big the shadow system was.
4. Credit became regionally stratified: rust belt and west starved, coast favored.
5. Introducing real default risk began the end of the decade-long credit bubble.
