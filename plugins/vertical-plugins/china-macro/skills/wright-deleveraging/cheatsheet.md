# Cheatsheet: *Grasping Shadows* (Wright, 2023)

## Decision rules
- **When Beijing squeezes a funding channel**, assume borrowers migrate. Find the new channel before calling the risk "contained".
- **When the PBOC tightens without hiking**, treat it as tightening anyway. Expect safe-asset selling and a possible market accident, and that policy reverses if one happens.
- **When defaults reach a new rung** of the ladder, raise systemic-risk odds. Watch the next rung, now LGFV public bonds.
- **When a sector's guarantee is in doubt**, read the leader, not the regulator. Under centralization, rhetoric like "for living, not speculation" caps support.
- **When credit tightens**, expect private firms, small banks and interior provinces to be hit first, and SOEs and the coast to be spared.
- **When TSF and bank assets diverge**, trust bank assets for shadow-credit swings.
- **When the GDP target is cut or dropped**, Beijing is doubling down. **When it rises to 6–7%**, deleveraging is being abandoned: expect a short boom and stress within 1–2 years.
- **When official GDP is flat but output, autos and white goods fall**, believe the output data.

## Tells and smells
- Starts rising while completions fall: developers are funding themselves with buyers' cash.
- A TSF methodology change during a slowdown: headline credit is being flattered.
- Perpetual-bond surges from SOEs: cosmetic deleveraging.
- A province's issuers shut out of the bond market after one local SOE default: geographic counterparty risk.
- SRBs used for refinancing (20–30%): local fiscal distress.
- LGFVs buying a large share of land (40–45% in 2022): propped-up land revenue.
- A new "authoritative personage" article: a major policy pivot is coming.
