# Glossary: *Grasping Shadows* (Wright, 2023)

**Asset management rules**: the 2018 rules covering banks, trusts, brokerages and funds. They banned guarantees, required mark-to-market, allowed one layer of intermediation and barred restricted sectors. (Ch 3)
**Authoritative personage (权威人士)**: an anonymous senior voice in *People's Daily*. The May 9, 2016 interview launched deleveraging and was presumed to be Liu He. (Ch 3)
**Bank assets**: the PBOC/CBRC "other depository corporations" balance sheet. A broader credit gauge than TSF because it includes claims on banks and NBFIs. (Ch 4)
**CBIRC**: China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission, formed in 2018 from the merger of the CBRC and CIRC. (Ch 3)
**Channel business**: banks routing funds through nonbanks (trusts, brokers, asset managers) to reach borrowers off-balance-sheet. (Ch 2)
**Claims on banks/NBFIs**: bank assets that concealed shadow loans. The swing from +RMB 9 trillion to −3.1 trillion marks the contraction. (Ch 4)
**Common prosperity**: the 2021 policy banner behind the tech and education crackdowns. (Ch 6, 7)
**CSFC**: China Securities Finance Corporation, the vehicle for the roughly RMB 2 trillion 2015 stock rescue. (Ch 2)
**Debt-for-equity swap**: the intended tool to cut SOE leverage. Banks avoided it. (Ch 3, 6)
**Deleveraging campaign (去杠杆)**: Beijing's 2016–19 multi-tool effort to cut systemic financial risk, chiefly shadow banking. (All)
**Financial deepening**: extending finance to new borrowers (SMEs, low-income households). Reversed by the crackdown. (Ch 6)
**Fiscalization**: converting local or LGFV debt into explicit central-government debt. (Ch 7)
**FSDC**: Financial Stability and Development Committee under the State Council (November 2017), led by Liu He. (Ch 3)
**Fund budget**: local governments' land-sale-based budget. First deficit in 2015, financed by SRBs. (Ch 5)
**Great ball of money**: surplus liquidity rotating between speculative markets (property, stocks, commodities, bonds). (Ch 2)
**Guo jin min tui (国进民退)**: "the state advances, the private sector retreats." (Ch 5)
**Implicit debt**: local borrowing outside official bonds (LGFVs, SOEs, PPPs, leasing, trusts) that will need fiscal repayment. (Ch 3)
**Implicit guarantee**: the belief that an asset (SOE or LGFV bond, bank liability, WMP) cannot default. (Ch 2)
**Interbank liabilities cap**: interbank liabilities may be at most one-third of total liabilities. NCDs were included from 2018. (Ch 2, 3)
**Investment receivables**: relabeled loans (trust beneficiary rights, directional AMPs). 18.3% of bank assets at the 2016 peak. (Ch 4)
**LGFV**: local government financing vehicle. ROA under 2%. Debt RMB 24 trillion (2016) to 57 trillion (2021). (Ch 2, 6)
**LMR**: Liquidity Matching Ratio (2018). Short wholesale funding (under 3 months) gets zero weight. (Ch 3)
**Macroprudential Assessment (MPA)**: the PBOC's quarterly A/B/C bank grading. Off-balance WMPs were added in December 2016. (Ch 3)
**Mark-to-market (WMPs)**: the requirement that WMP values reflect gains and losses, which ended deposit-like perception. (Ch 3)
**MLF**: Medium-term Lending Facility, one-year PBOC funding. (Ch 3)
**Moral hazard**: risk-taking under an assumed state backstop. The root cause of leverage. (Ch 2, 3, 6)
**NBFI**: nonbank financial institution (trusts, brokers, funds, insurers, finance companies, leasing, factoring). (Ch 2)
**NCD**: negotiable certificate of deposit. Uncollateralized interbank funding from end-2013. (Ch 2, 3)
**NFWC**: National Financial Work Conference, held every five years. The July 2017 conference created the FSDC. (Ch 3)
**OMO**: open market operations, the PBOC's reverse repos. (Ch 3)
**P2P**: peer-to-peer lending platforms. Peaked in 2015, collapsed 2017–18, all closed by 2020. (Ch 2, 3)
**Perpetual bond**: a callable bond booked as equity, used by SOEs to meet leverage targets cosmetically. (Ch 6)
**Presales**: selling homes before construction, with buyers mortgaged and escrow weakly enforced. (Ch 5)
**R-CAT**: Rhodium China Activity Tracker, a weighted index of 103 NBS output series. (Ch 5)
**Repo rate**: the rate on collateralized interbank borrowing. (Ch 2)
**Sealand Securities**: a December 2016 entrusted-bond dispute that triggered an interbank squeeze. (Ch 3)
**Shadow banking**: bank-like credit outside formal bank regulation. In China it is largely bank-sourced. The CBIRC (2020) list covers WMPs, SPVs, entrusted and trust loans, P2P and more. (Ch 2)
**Shares and other investments**: the PBOC sources-and-uses category capturing shadow credit. Peaked at 12.9% in February 2017. (Ch 4)
**Shock absorber**: the financial system's role in absorbing losses to avoid political instability. (Ch 1, 2)
**SRB**: local-government special revenue bond, for projects. 20–30% was used for refinancing in 2018–22. (Ch 5)
**Structured deposit**: a higher-yield deposit linked to derivatives, used as a WMP substitute until regulated. (Ch 3, 4)
**Swap bonds**: the RMB 12.2 trillion 2015–18 program replacing high-rate local shadow debt with bonds. (Ch 3)
**Three red lines**: August 2020 developer borrowing limits based on balance-sheet ratios. (Ch 3)
**Trust company**: a nonbank with a full lending license; 68 operate. The largest shadow channel. (Ch 2)
**TSF**: total social financing (aggregate financing to the real economy). Revised four times in 2018–19. (Ch 4, 6)
**Whack-a-mole**: regulating one channel only pushes activity into another. (Ch 2)
**WMP**: wealth management product, a short-term pooled investment that was implicitly guaranteed before 2018. (Ch 2)
